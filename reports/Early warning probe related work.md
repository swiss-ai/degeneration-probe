# Reframe ONWARD as adapted early-warning evaluation

The story holds up, but the paper has to change how it positions itself. Every part of ONWARD has a precedent. Seizure prediction, quickest change detection and ICU early warning already count alarms relative to an onset under a fixed false-alarm budget. LLM probe papers already set thresholds at a fixed false-positive rate on healthy data. One 2026 preprint (Itkin) already argues that token-level AUC hides how fast a monitor reacts after a hallucination begins. What no work found here does is score **token-level activation monitors relative to an annotated onset, both before it (warning) and after it (detection), under a per-response false-alarm budget at natural prevalence**, and then show that the choice of metric changes which probe gets selected. That combination is defensible as "to our knowledge, the first", but "we introduce an evaluation protocol" without naming its lineage is not. The scope question has a cleaner answer than "response level only". The draft should contrast classification metrics at any unit (response, chunk, sentence or token AUC and accuracy) with onset-aligned alarm metrics. Its evidence directly covers response-level AUC, and adding a token-level AUC column would let it cover the chunk and sentence evaluations of Word Salad Chopper, Duan et al. and SentGuard as well. On baselines, the degeneration subfield has no agreed set (Yu et al. state that none exist), so the paper does need its own. Three of the current ones map onto citable measures. LRS should be replaced by the causal repeated-n-gram rule that vLLM ships. The one missing baseline a monitor reviewer will ask for is a text-only classifier on the prefix. The citation audit found one claim that is wrong in kind (MERA cited for representation adaptation), four overstatements, stale venues, and the closest LoRA-plus-probe paper (Obeso et al.) missing entirely. The draft also has no main-text related-work section, which the paper-writing rules require.

Three of these change what the paper can say. **Itkin (2026)** makes nearly the same methodological argument for hallucination. He says token-level detectors "are evaluated as classifiers, by AUC over all tokens", and evaluates delay after the RAGTruth onset at a fixed false-alarm rate. Every detector catches under a third of onsets at that budget ([arXiv 2606.12476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12476)) [verified]. I read the full text for this report, and it marks out the difference clearly. Itkin's false-alarm rate is a per-token average run length (ARL₀ = 100 for α = 0.01). His features are black-box (text statistics, NLI, generator log-probabilities), not hidden states. And "a detector that misses or fires early" is charged the maximum delay, so warning before the onset gets no credit at all ([arXiv HTML 2606.12476](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12476)) [verified]. **SingStreamBench** annotates an `Unsafe_Start_Index` for each unsafe response, which is an onset label. Yet it scores guards with response-level AUC (the maximum score over all tokens) and token-level AUC, and defines no latency metric ([arXiv HTML 2608.30703](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30703)) [verified, first 100k characters]. Even a benchmark that has onset labels therefore falls back on pooled metrics, which is a clean example for the paper's argument. **Obeso et al.** is the nearest probe design (token-level linear and LoRA probes, streaming, five open models including Llama-3.1-8B). It scores entities by span-max AUC and never measures how early the probe fires ([arXiv 2509.03531](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03531v1)) [verified].

On degeneration itself, three papers need adding. **RADAR** reports the token position at which its harmful-state confidence crosses 50/70/90%, and says the 70% crossing precedes loop onset for naturally induced attacks. Its loops are mostly attack-induced, though, and its models are reasoning models ([arXiv 2609.38817](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38817v1)) [verified]. **Oladri et al.** probe residual states at fixed positions to predict budget non-convergence and get only AUC 0.61 on one model ([arXiv 2607.21433](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21433v1)) [verified]. That is a useful contrast for a probe that works on three models. **Ivgi et al.** frame repetition as a fallback under uncertainty ([arXiv 2407.06071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06071v2)) [verified], which helps explain why entropy rises before a loop and collapses once it starts. No public dataset with annotated loop onsets was found. A release of naturally occurring loops from instruct models, with judge onsets checked by humans, would therefore be a resource contribution in its own right.

The draft says "we introduce ONWARD". Checked component by component, most of the protocol is established practice in other fields, and the paper is stronger if it says so.

Kamran et al. are the most useful outside citation. A pooled discrimination score looks acceptable because the model fires once the outcome is already visible, and it looks close to random when only earlier predictions count. That is the same pattern the paper reports when the token-cap scorer reaches AUC 0.9991 without reading the model (draft, response-level metrics subsection). Citing it makes the saturation finding look like a known failure confirmed in a new setting, which reviewers will find more convincing than a claim of a new phenomenon. The point that max pooling ignores position needs no citation, but it is a one-line observation and should not be framed as a finding. The new evidence is empirical. Response-level metrics saturate across many monitors, and selecting on them costs warning coverage. According to the project notes, selecting on AUC costs 5.4× warning coverage on validation and 3.9× on test, on the recipe axis only. The draft does not report this yet.

The contrast with Duan et al. also needs one correction. The draft says prior probes "classify text that is already inside the repetitive pattern" (l. 676), but Duan do claim warning before textual repetition, so that sentence must exclude them. A fair contrast lists what Duan do differently. They use greedy decoding, balanced test sets of at least 50 loops and 50 non-loops per model, a threshold set as a multiple of the largest healthy CUSUM statistic instead of a fixed budget, an FPR of 0.24–0.34 at their operating point, and an onset defined by textual repetition, not by an annotation ([arXiv HTML 2601.05693](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05693v1)) [verified].

A defensible contribution sentence would read: "We adapt the event-based evaluation used in seizure prediction and quickest change detection, where alarms are counted relative to an onset under a fixed false-alarm budget, to per-token monitors of LLM generations. ONWARD fixes the share of healthy responses that may raise an alarm, and reports how much of the failure and of the tokens just before it a monitor flags, and how early its first alarm comes." Keeping the name ONWARD and the verb "introduce" is fine if this lineage sentence follows immediately. "To our knowledge, the first onset-aligned evaluation of token-level activation monitors that credits alarms before the onset" is supportable after one last check of the items in the final table. Avoid "first to measure lead time", "first to calibrate on healthy responses" and "a new persistence rule".

## Classification metrics at any unit hide timing, so frame the contrast that way

The question was whether to say the paper "only focuses on response level". Saying only that is weak, for two reasons. First, the closest degeneration work does not evaluate at the response level. Second, takeaway (1) in the story ("safety probes must be evaluated at token level") is not quite right, because a token-level AUC is also token level and still hides timing. The table shows what the closest works actually report.

| Work | Unit of evaluation | Metrics | Timing relative to an annotated onset? |
|---|---|---|---|
| WSC (Xie 2025) | chunk ("\n\n"-delimited), labels positive from the first detected repetition onward | accuracy, AUROC per chunk; task accuracy, tokens saved | No; chunk labels start at detection |
| Duan 2026 | sentence (statement loops) or token (numeral loops) classifier; response-level EDR/FPR | ACC/F1/AUC; EDR, FPR, lead in sentences and tokens | Lead relative to textual repetition, on balanced sets |
| Yu 2025 | response | accuracy, F1, FPR | No |
| SentGuard 2026 | sentence | Det@k, MFDS delay, streaming FPR | Sentence-level onsets, metric details not seen |
| Qwen3Guard 2025 | token, with sentence-level unsafe location | hit rate of the first flag inside the unsafe sentence (about 86%) | One hit rate, no budget, no lead |
| SCM 2025 | token scores turned into response decisions | macro-F1; share of the response read before the stop | Relative to response length only |
| Obeso 2025 | entity span (max over tokens) | AUC, recall at 10% FPR | No |
| SingStreamBench 2026 | response (max) and token | R-AUC, T-AUC | Onsets annotated but not used for timing |
| Itkin 2026 | token stream | delay after onset at fixed per-token ARL | Yes, but only after onset |

Sources for the rows are [WSC](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00536v1), [Duan](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05693v1), [Yu](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00416), [SentGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02041), [Qwen3Guard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14276), [SCM](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09996v3), [Obeso](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03531v1), [SingStreamBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30703) and [Itkin](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12476). SentGuard and Qwen3Guard are from abstracts and summaries only.

The common thread is not the unit. Everyone reports **classification quality at some unit, without an operating point tied to onset**. In this paper's setting, chunk-, sentence- or token-level AUC with labels "positive from onset onward" will very likely saturate for the same reason response AUC does. A loop runs to the 4,096-token cap, so the post-onset region (about 3,300 tokens in the PCA example of `fig:pca`, onset at 799) far outweighs a 128- or 256-token warning band. Any pooled token or chunk classification score is therefore mostly a detection score. This is a hypothesis, and the paper can test it cheaply because the per-token scores are already stored.

I recommend the following scope and wording. State that the paper contrasts **classification metrics, whether computed per response, chunk, sentence or token, with onset-aligned alarm metrics under a false-alarm budget**. Its main evidence is at the response level, because that is what deployed probe work reports and selects on (McKenzie, Goldowsky-Dill, SCM, Obeso's span-max, SingStreamBench's R-AUC). Add two cheap columns to the main metric figure or table: token-level AUC for detection labels (t ≥ o) and token-level AUC restricted to the warning band against healthy tokens. If the first saturates and the second does not, the argument covers WSC- and Duan-style evaluations without new training. If both behave like Warn_W, then the claim narrows to "pooled classification metrics", which is still useful. Either way, rephrase takeaway (1) as "safety probes should be evaluated relative to the onset, under a fixed false-alarm budget, and not only by classification metrics", and cite Itkin as having made the token-AUC point for hallucination detection.

## Keep three baselines, replace LRS, and add a text-only prefix classifier

WSC compares only against decoding penalties and brute-force truncation, Duan only against their own no-persistence ablation, and Yu et al. write that "no existing baselines address recurrent generation in LLMs" ([arXiv 2503.00416](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00416)) [verified]. What reviewers will object to is baselines that look invented. The fix is to tie each one to a published measure or a deployed tool, and to introduce it in the related-work paragraph it comes from. The text-only classifier matters most. Without it, the results show that probes beat lexical and confidence heuristics, but not that activations carry warning that the text does not. A small encoder or a 0.5B LM with a classification head would do. It should read the trailing window of generated text, be trained on the same onset-shifted labels and splits, and be calibrated with the same alarm rule. That is the comparison McKenzie et al. and Goldowsky-Dill et al. readers expect. If it cannot be run before the deadline, the limitations paragraph has to say plainly that all baselines are untrained. An LLM judge on prefixes at a stride is a nice extra but not needed for a workshop paper. Two items from the probing literature are cheap. A difference-of-means probe ([Marks and Tegmark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06824)) [verified] is optional. The existing permuted-onset control should be framed with Hewitt and Liang's control tasks ([arXiv 1909.03368](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.03368)) [verified]. Semantic entropy, P(True), SAE probes and logit-lens readouts are not needed. At the last layer, the logit lens is the output distribution that the entropy baseline already reads.

The minimal defensible set is therefore six rows. Never alarms and the token cap serve as references. One lexical row (windowed seq-rep-n), the causal n-gram rule, windowed entropy (with −log p if it is cheap), and the text-only prefix classifier make up the rest. The final-layer probe appears as the prior-work readout in the depth result. The compact β = 5% version belongs in the main text, because "the probe beats the baselines" is a main claim and the appendix table cannot support it there.

## Gated actions have support, but the 5% budget needs its cost spelled out

The "act on the alarm" motivation has broad support. Conditional Activation Steering applies a behaviour vector only when a condition vector read from hidden states fires, and reports harmless-refusal rates ([arXiv 2409.05907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05907)) [verified]. MERA steers only when a calibrated gain is significant and abstains otherwise ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/hedstrom25a.html)) [verified]. SafeSwitch switches on a safety head from a probe ([arXiv 2502.01042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01042)) [verified]. SCM, Qwen3Guard and Constitutional Classifiers halt streams. Backtracking emits a reset token and regenerates, cutting Llama-3-8B unsafe rates from 6.1% to 1.5% ([arXiv 2409.14586](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.14586)) [verified]. Probe-based early exit saves 24% of reasoning tokens ([arXiv 2504.05419](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05419v1)) [verified]. Entropy-triggered temperature changes exist as EDT ([arXiv 2403.14541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14541)) [verified], which matches the "change temperature" action, and Pipis et al. show that higher temperature reduces looping without removing its cause.

One note claimed that no paper acts on a repetition alarm. The degeneration note contradicts this, and the correct statement is narrower. WSC chops and regenerates on a probe alarm, LoopGuard prunes the KV cache, RADAR realigns attention, a loop-rescue detector falls back to FP16 in 2-bit models ([arXiv 2606.02011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02011)) [verified], and a CUSUM monitor triggers re-decoding in quantized reasoning models ([arXiv 2607.20129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20129)) [verified]. All of these act on an ongoing loop, mostly in reasoning models or under attack. None acts on a calibrated pre-onset warning in instruct models. This is the gap to claim, and it also exposes the paper's main weakness. The paper runs no intervention, so the motivation is untested. Say so in the limitations. Steering is also known to push activations off-manifold and cause "incoherent or repetitive outputs" ([arXiv 2607.05615](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05615)) [verified], which is a concrete argument for gating any steering on an alarm.

The 5% budget is looser than the 1% convention in probe monitoring (Goldowsky-Dill, McKenzie), and Itkin works at α = 0.01. It has direct precedents in agent monitoring, however, where Dubey reports results at a 5% budget and PrefixGuard at 10% ([arXiv 2608.02464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464), [arXiv 2605.06455](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06455)) [abstract; verified]. The cost-asymmetry argument is sound, but no paper found derives a budget for streaming monitors, so the paper is arguing it from its own reasoning. Three additions would make it concrete. First, report what a false alarm costs a healthy response under each action, for example the median number of tokens truncated by a stop, from the alarm positions on healthy validation responses. Obeso et al. are a rare paper that states this cost explicitly (about 50% of correct answers sacrificed). Second, keep 1% and 10% in the appendix so readers can compare with the probe convention. Third, state that the budget is relative to a stated healthy distribution. Constitutional Classifiers show benign FPR moving from 1.50% on MMLU-Chemistry to 26.05% on GPQA-Chemistry ([arXiv 2501.18837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18837)) [verified], so the held-out-domain false-alarm rate at the validation threshold should be reported. Calibrating per response, not per token, is the right unit, because a per-token rate compounds over 4,096 positions. None of the sources discuss this compounding, so the paper can make the point itself in one sentence.

The LoRA interpretation is plausible and has support, but the evidence in hand only supports "suggests". Obeso et al. are the direct precedent for jointly trained LoRA and a linear head. Prakash et al. find that fine-tuning strengthens an existing entity-tracking circuit and does not build a new one ([arXiv 2402.14811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14811)) [verified]. Jain et al.'s "wrapper" view points the same way [from memory, unverified]. The "coverage rises, early-warning rate unchanged" pattern did not appear in any source and looks new. On Apertus at β = 5%, Warn_256 rises from 0.207 to 0.486 while the lead moves only from −159 to −202 tokens (draft, `tab:baselines-vs-probes`). The challenge comes from Bailey et al., who show that fine-tuning can reshape representations so that a probe reads something different from the base model's state, driving harmfulness-probe recall from 100% to 0% ([arXiv 2412.09565](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.09565)) [verified]. A jointly trained adapter could likewise shape the representation toward the label. Two sentences would address this. One should state that the adapter runs only on the monitor's forward pass (or does not), and what that costs at inference. The other should report behaviour drift, as Obeso do with a KL term. The PCA figure shows a single response, and the text already admits it cannot settle separability.

My recommendation is to keep the frozen versus adapted result in the main text as one short research question. The released probes include both regimes, `fig:budget-bars-5pct` shows both, and the effect is large. Move the PCA figure and the mechanistic reading to the appendix, unless a quantitative measure across responses supports it (for example, warning-band separation of probe scores across all test responses). The training-strategy comparison belongs in the appendix, as planned.

## Conclusion

The literature check makes the paper's claim narrower and safer. The parts that look new in the draft (the budget, persistence and lead time) are borrowed, and the paper should borrow them openly. What is new is crediting alarms before an annotated onset for token-level activation monitors, together with evidence that the metric choice changes which probe a practitioner would ship. Itkin's hallucination paper, the closest competitor, gives warnings before the onset no credit by construction. SingStreamBench has onset labels and still reports pooled AUC. Both sharpen the paper's point instead of undercutting it. The same reasoning answers the scope question. The flaw is pooling for classification, whatever the unit, so the paper should name that flaw and test it with one cheap token-level AUC column instead of limiting itself to the response level.

The largest open risk is no longer the novelty claim. It is that every baseline is untrained and that no intervention is run. A text-only prefix classifier would turn "probes beat heuristics" into "activations warn earlier than text", and it is the single experiment most likely to move a reviewer. If there is no time for it, the limitations paragraph should name both gaps, and the false-alarm cost of each action should be stated in tokens, so that the 5% working point rests on a number instead of an argument.
