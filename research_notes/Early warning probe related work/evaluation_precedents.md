# Evaluation precedents for ONWARD (onset-aligned, false-alarm-budgeted evaluation of per-token monitors)

Scope of these notes. ONWARD has five parts: (a) a threshold calibrated so that a fixed fraction beta of healthy responses ever alarm, (b) optional persistence (m consecutive tokens above threshold), (c) metrics aligned to an annotated failure onset (post-onset coverage, Warn_W, early-warning rate, lead time), (d) the claim that response-level metrics (AUC, accuracy of the max-pooled score) ignore where a monitor fires and saturate near 1, and (e) applying all of this to token-level LLM monitors. The notes below check each part against prior work. "Same idea" means the prior work does the same operation with a different unit of time or domain. "Related idea" means it shares the motivation but measures something different.

Citations marked [verified] were checked against an abstract page, publisher page, or full text during this session. Citations marked [memory] were not checked in this session and need a quick lookup before they go into the paper.

## Q1. Precedents outside NLP: early classification, change-point detection, anomaly benchmarks, clinical early warning, seizure prediction, keyword spotting, biosurveillance

### Takeaway
Every individual ingredient of ONWARD has a well-established precedent outside NLP. Seizure prediction (sensitivity at a fixed maximum false prediction rate, with a prediction horizon relative to onset), quickest change detection (detection delay at a fixed false-alarm rate), and ICU event-based evaluation (event recall at a fixed alarm rate, counted only for alarms inside a window before the event) are the closest "same idea" precedents. Early time-series classification and NAB are "related idea" precedents, since they reward earliness but do not use a healthy-data false-alarm budget in the same way.

### Cited Findings

Seizure prediction (closest overall precedent)
- The seizure prediction characteristic (Winterhalder et al., 2003) evaluates a predictor by relating sensitivity to the false prediction rate together with two time intervals, the seizure prediction horizon (SPH, the minimum time between alarm and onset needed for intervention) and the seizure occurrence period (SOP, the window in which the seizure is expected after an alarm) — [Freiburg group papers summarising the framework](https://jeti.uni-freiburg.de/papers/clinNeurophysiol2006.pdf) [verified that the framework and its terms exist; original paper venue Epilepsy & Behavior 4(3):318-325, 2003, is memory]
- Results in this field are reported as sensitivity at a fixed false prediction rate per hour, for example 60% sensitivity at 0.15 false predictions per hour with SOP of 30 min and SPH of 10 min (Clinical Neurophysiology 117 (2006) 2399-2413) — [Freiburg, Clin Neurophysiol 2006](https://jeti.uni-freiburg.de/papers/clinNeurophysiol2006.pdf)
- A later critique in the same literature notes that high sensitivity is always achievable if a long prediction interval is allowed, and that performance must be compared against a random predictor — [Freiburg group paper](https://jeti.uni-freiburg.de/papers/long_horizons.pdf)
- Snyder, Echauz, Grimes and Litt (2008), "The statistics of a practical seizure warning system", J. Neural Eng. 5(4):392-401, doi 10.1088/1741-2560/5/4/004, derive the sensitivity of a chance predictor under a fixed limit on time spent in warning and propose the gap between algorithm and chance sensitivity as the test metric — [PMC2888045](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2888045) [verified]

Quickest change detection / change-point detection
- Basseville and Nikiforov, "Detection of Abrupt Changes: Theory and Application" (Prentice Hall, 1993), compare detectors by mean delay for detection (alarm time minus change time) at a fixed false-alarm probability or mean time between false alarms — [book PDF](https://members.cbio.mines-paristech.fr/~jvert/svn/bibli/local/Basseville1993Detection.pdf); [paper citing its criterion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.1237) [verified that the book and criterion exist]
- In the standard formulation false alarms are measured by the mean time to false alarm under the pre-change regime (the average run length, ARL) or its reciprocal, the false-alarm rate; Lorden's minimax criterion minimises the worst-case delay subject to a lower bound on mean time to false alarm — [Veeravalli & Banerjee, Quickest Change Detection, arXiv 1210.5552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.5552) [verified]

Clinical early warning
- Hyland et al. (2020), "Early prediction of circulatory failure in the intensive care unit using machine learning", Nature Medicine: the model predicts 90% of circulatory failure events, 82% of them more than 2 h in advance, with 0.05 alarms per patient-hour and AUPRC 0.63 — [BORIS Bern record](https://boris.unibe.ch/144988/) [verified abstract numbers; volume/pages 26:364-373 are memory]
- Its evaluation is event-based. An alarm fires when the score exceeds a threshold and no alarm was raised in the last 30 min, alarm precision is the share of alarms that are true, and event recall is the share of events captured, with the target defined over an 8 h horizon before the event — [Gumbsch, BIRS 2022 slides on longitudinal early warning](https://stats.birs.ca/workshops/2022/22w5055/files/Gumbsch/Thomas%20Gumbsch%20longitudinal%20early%20warning%20systems.pdf); [Hyland seminar abstract](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/chimera/events/2021/apr/stephanie-hyland-chimera-seminar-series)
- Tomašev et al. (2019), "A clinically applicable approach to continuous prediction of future acute kidney injury", Nature 572:116-119, doi 10.1038/s41586-019-1390-1: predicts 55.8% of inpatient AKI episodes with a lead time of up to 48 h at an operating point of 2 false alerts per true alert — [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/a-clinically-applicable-approach-to-continuous-prediction-of-future-acute-kidney-injury/); [PMC6722431](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6722431) [verified]
- PhysioNet/CinC Challenge 2019 (Reyna et al., Critical Care Medicine 48(2):210-217, doi 10.1097/CCM.0000000000004145) scored sepsis predictors with a utility function built to reward early predictions and penalise late or missed predictions and false alarms, relative to Sepsis-3 onset — [CinC 2019 abstract](https://www.cinc.org/2019/Program/accepted/412.html); [PhysioNet challenge page](https://www.physionet.org/content/challenge-2019/) [verified; the exact window constants (reward from 12 h before to 3 h after onset, peak at 6 h before) are memory]
- Wong et al. (2021), external validation of the Epic Sepsis Model, JAMA Internal Medicine: AUC 0.63, alerts on about 18% of hospitalized patients, missed 67% of sepsis cases, and caught only 7% of sepsis patients that clinicians had missed — [JAMA Netw](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2781313); [U. Michigan IHPI summary](https://ihpi.umich.edu/news/popular-sepsis-prediction-tool-less-accurate-claimed) [verified]
- Romero-Brufau, Huddleston, Escobar and Liebow (2015), "Why the C-statistic is not informative to evaluate early warning scores and what metrics to use", Critical Care, doi 10.1186/s13054-015-0999-1: recommends reporting sensitivity together with PPV, number needed to evaluate, or estimated alert rate, at each cutoff — [PMC4535737](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4535737/) [verified]

Anomaly detection benchmarks
- Lavin and Ahmad (2015), "Evaluating Real-time Anomaly Detection Algorithms: the Numenta Anomaly Benchmark", arXiv 1510.03336 (IEEE ICMLA 2015) — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1510.03336) [verified]
- NAB places a window around each labelled anomaly; detections inside the window earn a reward that decreases with delay through a scaled sigmoid, so earlier detections score more, and false positives outside windows are penalised (default weights A_TP = A_TN = 1, A_FP = A_FN = -1) — [review describing NAB scoring, arXiv 2105.12818](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.12818) [secondary source; check NAB Section 3 for exact window rule]
- Tatbul et al. (2018), "Precision and Recall for Time Series", NeurIPS 2018 (arXiv 1803.03639): range-based precision and recall with tunable existence reward, overlap size, cardinality, and a positional bias function, including a "front-end" bias that rewards detecting the beginning of an anomaly range — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/8f468c873a32bb0619eaeb2050ba45d1-Abstract.html); [author slides](https://people.csail.mit.edu/tatbul/talks/NeurIPS18_spotlight.pdf) [verified]
- Kim et al. (2022), "Towards a Rigorous Evaluation of Time-series Anomaly Detection", AAAI 2022 (arXiv 2109.05257): the common point-adjustment protocol (counting a whole anomaly segment as detected if any point in it is flagged) lets a random score reach state-of-the-art F1 and produces misleading rankings — [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/20680); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05257v1) [verified]

Early time-series classification
- Gupta, Gupta, Biswas and Dutta (2020), "Approaches and Applications of Early Classification of Time Series: A Review", IEEE Trans. Artificial Intelligence 1(1):47-61, doi 10.1109/TAI.2020.3027279, arXiv 2005.02595 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02595v2) [verified]
- Schäfer and Leser (2020), "TEASER: early and accurate time series classification", Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery 34:1336-1362; earliness is the fraction of the series observed before the decision, and methods are ranked by the harmonic mean HM = 2·Acc·(1 − Earliness)/(Acc + (1 − Earliness)) — [aeon TEASER docs](https://aeon-tsml.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_reference/auto_generated/aeon.classification.early_classification.TEASER.html); [Akasiadis et al. 2022 empirical comparison, arXiv 2203.01628](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01628) [verified]

Biosurveillance / activity monitoring
- Fawcett and Provost (1999), "Activity Monitoring: Noticing Interesting Changes in Behavior", KDD 1999, pp. 53-62 — [UT Austin listing](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~shivaram/readings/b2hd-FawcettProvost1999.html) [verified existence and venue]
- The AMOC (Activity Monitoring Operating Characteristic) curve plots expected time to detection against false-alarm rate across thresholds. It is described as an adaptation of ROC in which the true positive rate is replaced by detection delay, because ROC does not evaluate timeliness — [Jiang, Cooper & Neill, generalized AMOC](https://www.cs.nyu.edu/~neill/papers/gamoc.pdf); [ISDS repository](https://knowledgerepository.syndromicsurveillance.org/generalization-amoc-curve) [verified]

Keyword spotting
- Small-footprint keyword spotting is reported as false rejection rate at a fixed number of false alarms per hour, for example 97.71% accuracy at 0.5 FA/hour (Arik et al. 2017, arXiv 1703.05390) and 1.02% FRR at 1.0 FA/hour (Shan et al. 2018, arXiv 1803.10916) — [arXiv 1703.05390](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.05390); [arXiv 1803.10916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.10916) [verified]

### Inferences
- The false-alarm budget in ONWARD maps onto the seizure field's maximum false prediction rate, the change-point ARL constraint, the ICU alarm rate per patient-hour, and keyword spotting FA/hour. The one difference is the unit. ONWARD budgets the probability that a healthy response alarms at all (a per-sequence, or per-episode, false alarm probability), while these fields budget alarms per unit time. For responses of bounded length the two are closely related, and the per-sequence version is the natural choice when the deployment decision is "stop or flag this response".
- Early-warning rate (share of alarmed failing responses whose first alarm precedes onset) and lead time (median offset of first alarm from onset) are the same quantities as seizure-prediction sensitivity with SPH = 0 and as negative detection delay in change-point detection. Hyland et al. report the same kind of number ("82% identified more than 2 h in advance").
- Warn_W (share of the W tokens just before onset that are flagged) is closest to range-based recall over a window (Tatbul et al.) or to the NAB and PhysioNet windows before onset. It differs in that it scores token coverage inside a pre-onset window and not the existence or timing of a single detection. I found no precedent with exactly this definition.
- Post-onset coverage is the overlap-reward term of range-based recall (Tatbul et al.) restricted to the post-onset region. Kim et al.'s critique of point adjustment is a useful citation for why coverage (and not "any hit in the segment") is reported.
- Persistence (m consecutive tokens) is a standard debouncing or alarm-confirmation rule. CUSUM-style detectors and the 30 min silencing rule in Hyland et al. serve a similar purpose. It should be presented as a standard choice, not a contribution.

### Gaps
- I did not read the original Winterhalder et al. 2003 paper or the NAB scoring section in full; the exact NAB window-length rule and sigmoid constants come from a secondary review.
- Mormann et al. (2007), "Seizure prediction: the long and winding road", Brain 130:314-333 [memory], is the usual review to cite alongside Winterhalder; not checked here.
- Predictive maintenance: the C-MAPSS asymmetric scoring function (Saxena et al., PHM 2008) and the "prognostic horizon" metric (Saxena et al., 2010, "Metrics for offline evaluation of prognostic performance", IJPHM) [memory] are relevant but were not verified in this session.
- Early event detection in vision: Hoai and De la Torre, "Max-margin early event detectors" (CVPR 2012; IJCV 2014) [memory] use AMOC-style curves of normalised time to detection vs false positive rate; not verified here.
- Classic early classification: Xing, Pei and Keogh (2012), "Early classification on time series", Knowledge and Information Systems (ECTS) and Mori et al. (2017), DMKD [memory]; not verified here.
- The original keyword spotting reference (Chen, Parada, Heigold, ICASSP 2014) reports relative gains in its abstract; I could not confirm its FA/hour operating point from the excerpt.

## Q2. Precedents in NLP and LLM work

### Takeaway
In NLP, the eRisk early risk detection line (ERDE, latency-weighted F1) is an older precedent for latency-aware evaluation of streaming text classifiers. For LLMs, streaming safety classifiers (Qwen3Guard Stream, the Streaming Content Monitor) measure how early they stop a harmful response, and Qwen3Guard checks whether the first flagged token falls inside the annotated unsafe sentence, which is an onset-aligned check. Probe papers already calibrate thresholds to 1% FPR on unrelated healthy chat data. Two 2026 agent-monitoring papers (PrefixGuard, Dubey) use a false-alarm budget on successful runs plus a lead metric at the step level. None of the works found combine a token-level onset annotation, a per-response false-alarm budget on healthy responses, and onset-aligned coverage and lead metrics for LLM generations.

### Cited Findings

Early risk detection in text streams (NLP precedent)
- Losada and Crestani (2016, CLEF) introduced a depression test collection and the Early Risk Detection Error (ERDE), because F1 ignores how quickly a system detects risk. ERDE assigns costs to false positives and false negatives and a delay cost to true positives that grows (logistically) with the number of posts read before the decision — [Detecting early onset of depression, arXiv 2011.01695](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.01695); [eRisk 2018 overview, CEUR Vol-2125](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2125/invited_paper_1.pdf) [secondary sources describing the 2016 paper]
- Sadeque, Xu and Bethard (2018), "Measuring the Latency of Depression Detection in Social Media", WSDM 2018, pp. 495-503, doi 10.1145/3159652.3159725, critique ERDE and propose latency-weighted F1, with latency defined as the median number of posts read before a positive decision — [U. Arizona record](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/measuring-the-latency-of-depression-detection-in-social-media/); [Webis anthology](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/2018.wsdm_conference-2018.62) [verified venue; formula details from secondary source]

Streaming safety classifiers
- Qwen3Guard Technical Report (arXiv 2510.14276, 2025): Stream Qwen3Guard adds a token-level classification head for real-time monitoring during generation. On sentence-annotated data the first flagged token lands inside the human-annotated unsafe sentence about 86% of the time for direct responses; for reasoning traces it detects unsafe content within the first 128 tokens in about 66.8% of cases. It uses a debouncing rule in which tokens i and i−1 must both be flagged. The stream variant loses about 2 points of accuracy against the full-response variant — [arXiv 2510.14276](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14276) [verified via abstract and summaries]
- Li, Sheng, Yang, Zhang and Cao (2025), "From Judgment to Interference: Early Stopping LLM Harmful Outputs via Streaming Content Monitoring", NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2506.09996. They build FineHarm (29K prompt-response pairs with token-level labels) and a Streaming Content Monitor that reaches macro F1 ≥ 0.95 while seeing about 18% of tokens on average — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09996); [NeurIPS poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116186) [verified]
- In that paper timeliness is reported only as the position of termination as a fraction of the response ("about 50% of harmful responses are detected within the first 10% of tokens"). Token labels come from a POS heuristic, harmful words are "roughly evenly distributed across the responses", there is no annotated onset, and latency is not measured relative to one. The decision rule Harm@k flags a response once k tokens exceed a threshold θ, with θ and k chosen for best macro F1 on validation, not for a fixed false-alarm rate on benign responses — [arXiv 2506.09996v3 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09996v3) [verified]
- Constitutional Classifiers (Sharma et al., 2025, arXiv 2501.18837) include a streaming output classifier and report false-positive rates on benign domain questions (1.50% on MMLU-Chemistry, 26.05% on GPQA-Chemistry) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18837) [verified that the FPR numbers appear; streaming-specific timing metrics not found]

Token-level probes for LLMs
- Goldowsky-Dill et al. (2025), "Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes", ICML 2025 (PMLR v267), arXiv 2502.03407: with the threshold set to a 1% false positive rate on chat data unrelated to deception (Alpaca control responses), the probe catches 95-99% of deceptive responses; AUROC is 0.96-0.999 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.03407); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/goldowsky-dill25a.html) [verified]
- Obeso, Arditi, Ferrando, Freeman, Holmes and Nanda (2025), "Real-Time Detection of Hallucinated Entities in Long-Form Generation", arXiv 2509.03531: token-level linear or LoRA probes, entity score is the max token probability inside the entity span, evaluated mainly with AUC (e.g. 0.90 vs 0.71 for semantic entropy on Llama-3.3-70B) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03531) [verified]
- McKenzie et al., "Detecting High-Stakes Interactions with Activation Probes", NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2506.10805, note that max pooling over per-token predictions biases probes towards the single most high-stakes token — [NeurIPS paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/b9501301a7a0f99e57406cc901f4e3ce-Paper-Conference.pdf) [verified via search snippet]
- Chrabąszcz et al. (2026), "Monitoring the Internal Monologue: Probe Trajectories Reveal Reasoning Dynamics", arXiv 2605.18549: per-sample labels only, AUROC as the primary metric, plus AUROC as a function of the share of chain-of-thought tokens seen; no onset annotation, no lead time, no false-alarm calibration — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18549) [verified]

Span-level hallucination benchmarks
- RAGTruth (Niu et al., ACL 2024, arXiv 2401.00396) scores span-level hallucination detection by character-level precision, recall and F1 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.00396) [verified]
- Mu-SHROOM (SemEval-2025 Task 3) ranks systems by character-level IoU between gold and predicted hallucinated spans and by a probability-correlation score — [MSA system paper, arXiv 2505.20880](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.20880); [ODESIA task page](https://portal.odesia.uned.es/en/tarea/mu-shroom-multilingual-shared-task-hallucinations-and-related-observable-overgeneration) [verified]

LLM agent monitors with false-alarm budgets (closest 2026 work)
- Huang et al. (2026), "PrefixGuard: Online Failure Warning and Trace-Grounded Diagnosis for LLM Agents", arXiv 2605.06455 (v2 Sep 2026). Lead is "how early the first alert arrives", false-alarm rate is "the share of successful runs it alerts on", and the threshold is set by split-conformal calibration on successful runs at a nominal FAR budget of 10%. Labels come from terminal outcomes; there is no step-level onset annotation — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06455) [verified]
- Dubey (2026), "Real-Time Detection and Repair of LLM Agent Failures", arXiv 2608.02464: monitors trained only on healthy runs, with CUSUM alarms, detect 0.71 of failures at a 5% false-alarm budget (AUROC 0.872); the paper mentions a "post-onset horizon" but the abstract does not define how onset is labelled — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464v1) [verified abstract]

### Inferences
- The per-response false-alarm budget on healthy responses is already used in LLM probing (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 1% FPR on Alpaca) and in 2026 agent monitors (PrefixGuard 10%, Dubey 5%). ONWARD should cite these and present the budget as standard practice, not as new.
- The onset-aligned part for LLM generations is the least covered. Qwen3Guard's "first flagged token inside the annotated unsafe sentence" is the nearest onset-aligned check, but it is a single hit rate, without lead time, pre-onset warning coverage, or a fixed false-alarm budget. SCM measures earliness relative to response length, not to onset.
- eRisk is the right NLP-history citation for "standard classification metrics ignore detection delay in streaming text". It works at the level of posts in a user history, not tokens in one generation.
- Persistence in ONWARD has direct LLM precedents in Qwen3Guard's two-token debouncing and in SCM's Harm@k rule.

### Gaps
- "Doomed from the Start: Early Abort of LLM Agent Episodes via a Recall-Controlled Probe Cascade" (arXiv 2607.06503) appeared in search results but I did not open it; it may use a related budgeted early-abort evaluation.
- A 2026 Journal of Biomedical Informatics paper ("Real-time hallucination detection and intervention in medical LLMs via calibrated hidden-state probes", ScienceDirect PII S153204642600105X) frames token-time critical-point detection with Safe/AtRisk/Hallucinating states. The publisher page returned 403, so title, authors and metrics are unverified. It should be checked, since it may be the closest token-level precedent.
- I found no LLM work that reports "detection latency" for content moderation relative to an annotated harm onset in tokens, beyond Qwen3Guard's hit rate.
- I did not check early-exit papers for reasoning models (e.g. probes predicting answer correctness for early stopping) in detail; they evaluate accuracy vs tokens saved, which is a compute trade-off and not an onset-aligned warning.

## Q3. Critiques of AUC and accuracy, and fixed-FPR metrics

### Takeaway
There is a strong literature arguing that AUC is uninformative for deployment decisions under rare events and that detectors should be compared at a fixed low false-positive rate. The clinical early-warning critique (Romero-Brufau et al.) and the security convention of TPR at low FPR (Carlini et al.) are the two most direct supports for ONWARD's argument against response-level AUC.

### Cited Findings
- Romero-Brufau et al. (2015) argue AUROC/C-statistic is not suited to pre-implementation evaluation of early warning scores because deterioration is rare (prevalence about 0.02 per patient-day); at low prevalence a 3% drop in specificity can halve PPV — [PMC4535737](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4535737/) [verified]
- Saito and Rehmsmeier (2015), PLoS ONE 10(3):e0118432, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0118432: ROC plots can mislead on imbalanced data because of a wrong intuitive reading of specificity; precision-recall plots are more informative — [PMC4349800](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4349800/) [verified]
- Carlini et al. (2022), "Membership Inference Attacks From First Principles", IEEE S&P 2022, arXiv 2112.03570: attacks should be evaluated by TPR at low FPR, and most prior attacks look weak under this test even though they look fine on average-case metrics — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03570v1); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9833649) [verified]
- Goldowsky-Dill et al. (2025) report recall at 1% FPR on control data as the headline number for deception probes, alongside AUROC — [arXiv 2502.03407](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.03407) [verified]
- Kim et al. (2022) show that an evaluation protocol can make a random scorer look state of the art and produce misleading method rankings in time-series anomaly detection — [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/20680) [verified]
- Wong et al. (2021) show a deployed early-warning model with AUC 0.63 that alerts on about 18% of patients, which is the alert-burden view that AUC does not show — [JAMA Netw](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2781313) [verified]

### Inferences
- These works argue that AUC hides the operating point. ONWARD's argument is a second, separate point, that response-level AUC also hides timing. Both points should be made, and the first one should be credited to this literature.
- TPR at fixed FPR is common in watermark and machine-text detection (e.g. Krishna et al., NeurIPS 2023, "Paraphrasing evades detectors of AI-generated text" [memory]) and could be cited as further evidence that fixed-FPR reporting is standard in ML security; not verified here.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session (all [memory]): Davis and Goadrich (2006), "The relationship between precision-recall and ROC curves", ICML; McClish (1989), "Analyzing a portion of the ROC curve", Medical Decision Making (partial AUC); Hand (2009), "Measuring classifier performance: a coherent alternative to the area under the ROC curve", Machine Learning; Lobo et al. (2008), "AUC: a misleading measure of the performance of predictive distribution models", Global Ecology and Biogeography.

## Q4. Does prior work argue that pooled or response-level scores hide the timing of a monitor?

### Takeaway
Yes outside LLMs, in several forms. The AMOC literature states that ROC does not evaluate timeliness, eRisk states that F1 ignores delay, and Kamran et al. (2024) show empirically that a sepsis model's AUROC falls from 0.62 to 0.47 when only predictions made before clinicians start treatment are counted. For LLM token-level monitors, I found no paper that makes the argument explicitly. The closest is Chrabąszcz et al. (2026), who argue full trajectories carry more information than single predictions but still evaluate with AUROC.

### Cited Findings
- Kamran, Tjandra, Heiler et al. (2024), "Evaluation of Sepsis Prediction Models before Onset of Treatment", NEJM AI, doi 10.1056/AIoa2300032: on 77,582 patients (4.9% sepsis) with predictions every 20 min, the Epic Sepsis Model had AUROC 0.62 for predictions before sepsis criteria were met and 0.47 for predictions before the first treatment indicator, suggesting the model partly reacts to clinicians' own actions — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10522981); [U. Michigan CSE story](https://cse.engin.umich.edu/stories/widely-used-ai-tool-for-early-sepsis-detection-may-be-cribbing-doctors-suspicions) [verified via record and secondary summaries]
- AMOC: "A ROC curve does not evaluate the timeliness of detection which is critical in syndromic surveillance"; AMOC replaces TPR with detection delay — [Jiang, Cooper & Neill](https://www.cs.nyu.edu/~neill/papers/gamoc.pdf) [verified]
- eRisk: ERDE was introduced because F1 ignores how quickly a system detects risk — [arXiv 2011.01695](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.01695) [secondary]
- Tatbul et al. (2018): classical point-based precision and recall are a special case (unit ranges, flat positional bias) of their range-based metrics, and do not capture where in a range a detection falls — [author slides](https://people.csail.mit.edu/tatbul/talks/NeurIPS18_spotlight.pdf) [verified]
- Chrabąszcz et al. (2026) argue that "future model behavior is more distinguishable when examined over the full trajectory than from a single static prediction", but report AUROC throughout and do not annotate onset — [arXiv 2605.18549](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18549) [verified]
- Search results for a 2026 anomaly-detection audit noted that an apparently large lead time mostly reflected where an alarm sat relative to a retrospectively defined boundary, i.e. lead time depends on how onset is labelled — [arXiv 2601.11415](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.11415) [seen only in a search summary; not opened]

### Inferences
- Kamran et al. is the strongest analogue of ONWARD's saturation claim. A model can look good on a pooled discrimination metric because it fires once the outcome is already visible, and the same model looks near random when only pre-onset predictions count. ONWARD's finding that max-pooled response AUC saturates while Warn_W separates monitors is the same phenomenon in token space. Citing it makes the claim look like an established pattern applied to a new setting, which is more convincing for reviewers.
- The specific claim that max-pooled response-level AUC is invariant to the position of the alarm inside the response is a simple mathematical observation (max is permutation invariant over positions). It needs no citation, but the paper should not present it as a deep finding. The empirical part (saturation near 1 across many monitors, and that the ranking by AUC does not transfer while the ranking by Warn does) is the new evidence.

### Gaps
- I did not find any LLM paper that compares response-level AUC with an onset-aligned metric on the same monitors. This is the gap ONWARD can claim, with the caveat that the 2026 agent-monitor literature is moving quickly and the unread items listed in Q2 Gaps should be checked first.

## Q5. How the paper should phrase its contribution, and which references are essential

### Takeaway
ONWARD should be presented as an adaptation of event-based early-warning evaluation (seizure prediction, quickest change detection, ICU event-based alarms) to token-level LLM monitors. The new parts are the onset-aligned token metrics for LLM generations (in particular Warn_W), the per-response false-alarm budget used together with them, and the empirical demonstration that response-level metrics saturate and give rankings that do not transfer. The false-alarm budget, persistence, lead time, and the general critique of AUC should be credited to prior work.

- Instead of "We introduce ONWARD, a new evaluation protocol", write something like "We adapt the event-based evaluation used for seizure prediction and clinical early warning, where alarms are counted relative to an annotated onset under a fixed false-alarm budget, to per-token monitors of LLM generations.
