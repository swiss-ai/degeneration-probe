# Monitors for in-generation LLM failures: probes, streaming classifiers and early-warning signals

Conventions used in every entry below.
- Type 1 = response classifier (judges a finished response or a full sequence).
- Type 2 = failure detector (flags a failure once it is under way, inside the generation).
- Type 3 = early-warning monitor (fires before the failure starts, or before the response is written).
- "Streaming" = the monitor produces a score while the response is being generated (per token, per sentence or per step).
- Entries marked **[verified]** were checked against the arXiv abstract page, the venue page or a fetched full text during this session. Entries marked **[known, not re-fetched]** are well-known papers whose arXiv id and main content I am confident about, but which I did not open in this session; the report writer should treat their evaluation details as needing a quick check.

## Q1. Hallucination probes and streaming hallucination detection: which evaluate at token/span level, which at response level?

### Takeaway
Most hallucination probes (SAPLMA, HaloScope, semantic entropy probes, Orgad et al., FactCheckmate, Ji et al.) are scored as response- or answer-level classifiers with AUROC or accuracy. Only a small group scores spans or tokens (CH-Wang et al., Obeso et al., MIND, RAGTruth-based work), and among these only one 2026 preprint (Itkin) evaluates detection delay relative to an annotated onset under a fixed false-alarm rate, which is the closest existing work to the paper's protocol.

### Cited Findings

**Response / answer-level (mostly Type 1, some Type 3 because they read the prompt only)**
- **SAPLMA, Azaria & Mitchell 2023, "The Internal State of an LLM Knows When It's Lying" (arXiv 2304.13734, EMNLP Findings 2023).** Failure: false statements. Signal: hidden-layer activations of the model reading a statement. Type 1. Not streaming. Evaluation: statement-level classification accuracy on true/false datasets. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13734)
- **HaloScope, Du et al. 2024 (arXiv 2409.17504, NeurIPS 2024).** Failure: hallucinated answers. Signal: a subspace of hidden-state embeddings estimated from unlabeled generations. Type 1. Not streaming. Evaluation: response-level AUROC on TruthfulQA, TriviaQA, CoQA, TydiQA. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17504)
- **INSIDE / EigenScore, Chen et al. 2024 (arXiv 2402.03744, ICLR 2024).** Failure: hallucinated answers. Signal: covariance of sentence embeddings across several sampled responses. Type 1. Not streaming. Evaluation: response-level AUROC. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03744)
- **Semantic entropy, Farquhar et al. 2024 (Nature).** Failure: confabulations. Signal: output text of many samples clustered by meaning. Type 1. Not streaming. Evaluation: response-level AUROC and rejection-accuracy curves. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)
- **Semantic Entropy Probes (SEPs), Kossen et al. 2024 (arXiv 2406.15927).** Failure: hallucination, via predicting semantic entropy. Signal: hidden states of a single generation, either the second-last token (SLT) or the token before generation (TBG). The TBG variant is an early-warning monitor (Type 3) because it predicts from the last input token "without generating any novel tokens"; SLT is Type 1. Evaluation: response-level AUROC, roughly 0.7 to 0.95 across scenarios, TBG slightly below SLT. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15927)
- **Orgad et al. 2025, "LLMs Know More Than They Show" (arXiv 2410.02707, ICLR 2025).** Failure: wrong answers. Signal: activations at the "exact answer tokens". Type 1. Not streaming. Evaluation: response-level AUROC; shows truthfulness signal is concentrated in high-information answer tokens and generalises poorly across tasks. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02707)
- **Ji et al. 2024, "LLM Internal States Reveal Hallucination Risk Faced With a Query" (arXiv 2407.03282, BlackboxNLP 2024).** Failure: hallucination risk on a query. Signal: last-token hidden state of the query. Type 3 (before any response). Evaluation: query-level accuracy, 84.32% average over 15 NLG tasks / 700+ datasets. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03282)
- **FactCheckmate, Alnuhait et al. (arXiv 2410.02899, EMNLP Findings 2025).** Failure: hallucination. Signal: hidden states over the input. Type 3, with intervention (a learned model shifts hidden states when a hallucination is predicted). Evaluation: detection accuracy above 70% on QA datasets, outputs 34.4% more factual after intervention. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02899v2); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/dashboard/2025.findings-emnlp.663.bib)
- **Kadavath et al. 2022, "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know" (arXiv 2207.05221).** Failure: wrong answers. Signal: model's own predicted P(IK) from the question. Type 3. Evaluation: calibration and AUROC at question level. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)
- **Cencerrado et al. 2025, "No Answer Needed: Predicting LLM Answer Accuracy from Question-Only Linear Probes" (arXiv 2509.10625, ICLR 2026).** Failure: wrong answers. Signal: residual-stream activations after the question, before any answer. Type 3. Evaluation: question-level prediction of correctness; probes trained on trivia transfer to other knowledge datasets, beat verbalised confidence, saturate at middle layers, but fail on math reasoning. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10625)
- **KEEN, Gottesman & Geva 2024, "Estimating Knowledge in LLMs Without Generating a Single Token" (arXiv 2406.12673).** Failure: lack of knowledge about an entity. Signal: hidden states of the subject entity. Type 3. Evaluation: correlation with QA accuracy and factuality. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12673)
- **Ferrando et al. 2025, "Do I Know This Entity?" (arXiv 2411.14257, ICLR 2025).** Failure: hallucination about unknown entities. Signal: SAE latents for entity recognition. Type 3 (read from the entity in the prompt). Evaluation: mechanistic analysis plus refusal steering, not a monitor benchmark. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14257)
- **Snyder, Moisescu & Zafar 2024, "On Early Detection of Hallucinations in Factual Question Answering" (arXiv 2312.14183, KDD 2024).** Failure: hallucinated answers in open QA. Signal: softmax outputs, Integrated-Gradients input attributions, self-attention and fully connected activations. Type 3 in part, since they report that signals on tokens before the hallucination already predict it. Evaluation: response-level AUROC up to 0.80. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2312.14183); [Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/publications/on-early-detection-of-hallucinations-in-factual-question-answering)

**Span / token / sentence level (Type 2, streaming or streamable)**
- **CH-Wang, Van Durme, Eisner & Kedzie, "Do Androids Know They're Only Dreaming of Electric Sheep?" (arXiv 2312.17249, ACL Findings 2024).** Failure: hallucination in grounded generation (contradicting the source). Signal: hidden states, probes per token. Type 2. Streaming-compatible. Evaluation: span-level hallucination annotation on organic and synthetic outputs; reaches 95% of peak performance as early as layer 4; probes generalise poorly across tasks and from synthetic to organic errors. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.17249v1); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2024.findings-acl.260)
- **MIND, Su et al. 2024, "Unsupervised Real-Time Hallucination Detection based on the Internal States of LLMs" (arXiv 2403.06448, ACL Findings 2024).** Failure: hallucination. Signal: hidden states, classifier trained on pseudo-labels from truncated Wikipedia articles. Type 2. Real-time during generation. Also introduces the HELM benchmark, which records internal states during inference for six LLMs. Evaluation granularity reported as sentence- and passage-level (from memory; the abstract only says it beats prior detectors). **[verified existence; granularity from memory]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06448v2)
- **Obeso, Arditi, Ferrando, Freeman, Holmes & Nanda 2025, "Real-Time Detection of Hallucinated Entities in Long-Form Generation" (arXiv 2509.03531).** Failure: fabricated entities (names, dates, citations). Signal: linear and LoRA probes (value head) on residual-stream activations at layer ~0.95 depth, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B, Gemma-2-9B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Mistral-Small-24B. Type 2, streaming. Labels come from a web-search annotation pipeline (80.6% recall on injected errors, 15.8% false-positive rate on unchanged entities). Evaluation: entity-level (span-max of token scores) AUC and recall at 10% FPR; AUC 0.90 vs 0.71 for semantic entropy on Llama-3.3-70B. Does **not** report token-level AUC and does **not** measure latency or how early the probe fires. Includes a selective-answering intervention that halts generation when any token exceeds a threshold. **[verified]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03531v1)
- **Lookback Lens, Chuang et al. 2024 (arXiv 2407.07071, EMNLP 2024).** Failure: contextual hallucination. Signal: per-head ratio of attention on context vs generated tokens. Type 2, chunk/span level. Used for classifier-guided decoding that samples candidate chunks (9.6% fewer hallucinations on XSum). **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07071)
- **Varshney et al. 2023, "A Stitch in Time Saves Nine" (arXiv 2307.03987).** Failure: hallucinated sentences. Signal: token logit confidence of key concepts. Type 2, sentence-by-sentence during generation, with mitigation before continuing. Evaluation: sentence-level detection recall/precision. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03987)
- **SelfCheckGPT, Manakul et al. 2023 (arXiv 2303.08896).** Failure: non-factual sentences. Signal: text consistency across samples. Type 1/2 (sentence-level, post-hoc). Evaluation: sentence-level AUC-PR plus passage-level correlation. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08896)
- **RAGTruth, Niu et al. 2024 (arXiv 2401.00396).** A word/span-level annotated hallucination corpus for RAG. It is the dataset Itkin (below) uses to validate an onset model. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00396)
- **Fadeeva et al. 2024, "Fact-Checking the Output of LLMs via Token-Level Uncertainty Quantification" (arXiv 2403.04696).** Failure: false claims. Signal: token-level output probabilities (claim-conditioned probability). Type 2, claim level. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04696)
- **Lu et al. 2026, "Streaming Hallucination Detection in Long Chain-of-Thought Reasoning" (arXiv 2601.02170, ACL Findings 2026).** Failure: hallucination inside long CoT. Signal not specified in the abstract. Type 2, streaming. Treats hallucination as an evolving latent state; step-level judgments are combined into a cumulative prefix-level signal. The abstract does not report latency or earliness metrics. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02170); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.findings-acl.1064/)
- **Itkin 2026, "Quickest Detection of Hallucination Onset: Delay Bounds and Learned CUSUM Statistics" (arXiv 2606.12476, preprint).** Failure: onset of hallucination in streamed text. Signal: per-token learned scores from features (the abstract does not say activations vs logits). Type 2, streaming. Explicitly argues that token-level detectors "are evaluated as classifiers, by AUC over all tokens" and should instead be judged by detection delay at a fixed false-alarm rate. Validated a two-state Markov onset model on RAGTruth. Lorden lower bound about 1.3 tokens at false-alarm rate 0.01; a learned recurrent detector takes 11 to 13 tokens vs 31 for a linear per-token baseline among caught onsets, but every detector catches under a third of onsets at that budget; "recall-honest" delay is 56 to 66 tokens. **[verified, single-author preprint, no venue]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12476)
- **SingProbe Technical Report, Sing Team 2026 (arXiv 2608.30703).** Probe on base-model hidden states during decoding that produces query-intent, response-safety and hallucination-risk signals continuously; integrated into SGLang/vLLM for 29 open models with under 0.5% serving overhead. Type 2. Also introduces SingStreamBench (see Q2). **[verified abstract]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30703)

### Inferences
- The field's default unit for hallucination probes is the answer or response, and most "early" hallucination work (SEP-TBG, Ji et al., FactCheckmate, Cencerrado et al., KEEN) is early only in the sense of reading the prompt before the answer. That is a single prediction per query, not a running monitor with a timing evaluation.
- Obeso et al. is the strongest token-level probe paper, but it scores entities by span-max and never measures how early the alarm comes. The paper under review can position itself as adding the timing axis that Obeso et al. leave out.
- Itkin (2026) makes almost the same methodological argument as the paper (onset-relative delay, fixed false-alarm budget, response/token AUC hides timing) for hallucination. It must be cited and the paper should explain how its own protocol differs (e.g., degeneration with a clear onset, linear probes on 8B models, early warning before onset vs quickest detection after onset).

### Gaps
- I did not confirm MIND's exact evaluation granularity or metrics from the full text.
- I did not find which signal (activations or logits) Itkin's detector reads; the abstract does not say.
- I did not search for vision-language token-level probes in depth (NVIDIA UniProbe appeared in a search result, but I did not verify it).

## Q2. Harmful content / jailbreak streaming monitors: do any evaluate how early they fire relative to the first harmful token?

### Takeaway
Streaming harm monitors are now common (Constitutional Classifiers, SCM, Qwen3Guard-Stream, Kelp, SentGuard, StreamGuard, SingProbe), and several report earliness. Most measure earliness as the position of the stop relative to response length or as "caught within the first k sentences", not as delay from an annotated first harmful token. Qwen3Guard (exact hit rate on sentence-annotated onset), StreamGuard ("on-time intervention" on onset-located test data) and SingStreamBench (benign prefixes vs emerging unsafe content) come closest to onset-relative evaluation, and StreamGuard is the one explicit Type 3 forecaster.

### Cited Findings
- **Constitutional Classifiers, Sharma et al. 2025 (arXiv 2501.18837).** Failure: universal jailbreaks for CBRN content. Signal: separate fine-tuned classifier models on input and output. The output classifier is a streaming classifier that scores each token prefix and can halt the output mid-stream; it was trained with a mix of per-token and cumulative-maximum objectives (these streaming details are from my knowledge of the paper; the Anthropic blog post does not describe them). Type 2. Evaluation: jailbreak success rate on 10,000 synthetic prompts (86% to 4.4%), refusal rate on production traffic (+0.38%), compute overhead (+23.7%), plus large-scale human red teaming. No onset-relative timing metric reported in the blog. **[blog verified; streaming detail known, not re-fetched]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/constitutional-classifiers); [dblp](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/pid/25/11096.html)
- **Constitutional Classifiers++, Cunningham et al. (arXiv 2601.04603, ICLR 2026).** Signal: linear probes on the policy model's activations as a first stage, then a probe plus external-classifier ensemble on flagged exchanges; exchange classifiers judge outputs in full conversation context. Type 1/2 in a cascade. Probe cost estimated at about 377K FLOPs per token for an all-layer probe on Gemma 3 27B, "effectively zero" marginal cost for per-token prediction. Evaluation: compute reduction (40x in the arXiv version, 5.4x in the ICLR poster), production refusal rate (0.05% / 0.036%), red-teaming hours or queries. Logit smoothing and a weighted loss reported as important for probes. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04603); [ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/4f697305ef1f868ad77c3c0027989a6f-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Kramár et al. 2026, "Building Production-Ready Probes For Gemini" (arXiv 2601.11516, Google DeepMind).** Failure: cyber-offensive misuse. Signal: activation probes with new architectures for long context. Type 1/2. Evaluation: robustness to multi-turn, long-context and adaptive red-teaming shifts; probes paired with prompted classifiers; informed deployment in Gemini. No timing metric reported in the abstract. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11516)
- **SCM, Li et al. 2025, "From Judgment to Interference: Early Stopping LLM Harmful Outputs via Streaming Content Monitoring" (arXiv 2506.09996, NeurIPS 2025).** Signal: a separate Qwen2.5 model (0.5B to 7B) reading prompt plus generated prefix, with a token scorer. Type 2. FineHarm dataset: 29K prompt-response pairs with token labels made heuristically (LLM labels sentences, then nouns/verbs/adjectives in harmful sentences are marked harmful). Evaluation: response-level macro F1 under "Harm@k" (stop after k flagged tokens, threshold and k tuned on validation); macro F1 at least 0.95 after seeing about 18% of tokens on average; over 80% of harmful responses caught within the first 30% of tokens. Earliness is measured as stop position over response length, **not** as lag after the first harmful token; false-alarm rate is discussed qualitatively but not formally defined. **[verified, full text]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09996v3); [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116186)
- **Qwen3Guard Technical Report (arXiv 2510.14276).** Stream Qwen3Guard adds token-level heads on the last layer of a Qwen3 guard backbone that label each streamed token safe / unsafe / controversial, with a debounce rule (two consecutive flagged tokens). Type 2. Evaluation: on sentence-level annotated data, exact hit rate about 86% for direct responses (the flag lands on the annotated unsafe position); for thinking traces, unsafe content caught within the first 128 tokens in about 66.8% of cases; safety classification about 2 points below the generative guard. Used in a detect-rollback-intervene framework (CARE). **[verified, abstract and summaries]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14276v1)
- **Kelp, Li et al. 2025, "A Streaming Safeguard for Large Models via Latent Dynamics-Guided Risk Detection" (arXiv 2510.09694).** Signal: intermediate hidden states of the protected model, read by a ~20M-parameter Streaming Latent Dynamics head trained with a loss that enforces monotone harm predictions under a benign-then-harmful prior. Type 2. Introduces StreamGuardBench, where each protected model generates its own responses. Evaluation: average F1 in response-level and streaming settings (+15.61% over guardrails and plug-in probes), under 0.5 ms added per token. **[verified abstract]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09694)
- **SentGuard, Yu et al. 2026, "Sentence-Level Streaming Guardrails for LLMs" (arXiv 2606.02041).** Signal: a trained guardrail model run at sentence boundaries with a waiting buffer. Type 2, sentence-level. Introduces StreamSafe with per-sentence annotations over 8 harm categories, covering reasoning and response. Evaluation: Det@k (caught within first k sentences; 90.5% within two sentences), a delay-style measure (MFDS), and streaming false-positive rate (7.41%). Argues token-level guards are unstable and response-level guards are too late. **[verified abstract; metric definitions not seen]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02041)
- **StreamGuard, Kavumba et al. 2026, "Predict, Don't React: Value-Based Safety Forecasting for LLM Streaming" (arXiv 2604.03962).** Signal: a guard model reading the partial prefix and predicting the expected harmfulness of likely continuations, trained with Monte Carlo rollouts (no exact boundary labels needed). **Type 3**, explicitly framed against "boundary detection" of the first already-unsafe prefix. Evaluation: miss rate 7.9% to 4.9% vs Qwen3Guard-Stream-8B-strict on the QwenGuardTest response_loc streaming benchmark, "on-time intervention" 92.6% vs 89.9%, F1 97.5. False alarms not reported in the abstract. **[verified abstract]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03962)
- **SingStreamBench (in SingProbe report, arXiv 2608.30703).** Benchmark for streaming guardrails that checks whether a guard "remain[s] inactive on benign prefixes while promptly detecting emerging unsafe content". A search summary says it annotates cumulative sentence-level prefixes to mark the unsafe onset because response-level labels cannot show when harm appears; I could not confirm that wording from the abstract. **[abstract verified; onset-annotation detail from search summary only]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30703)
- **Xie, Song, Zhou, Huang, Song & Ma 2024, "Online Safety Analysis for LLMs: a Benchmark, an Assessment, and a Path Forward" (arXiv 2404.08517).** Benchmark of 8 online (during-generation) safety analysis methods on 8 LLMs across QA, continuation, translation and code, with 5 metrics. A pilot study reports that most unsafe outputs can be detected within the first 25% of generated content. **[verified abstract; 25% figure from search snippet]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08517)
- **Schirmer, Jazbec, Timans, Naesseth, Waldron & Nalisnick 2026, "Online Safety Monitoring for LLMs" (arXiv 2607.02510, ICML 2026 Hypothesis Testing Workshop).** Signal: an external verifier score per step (Qwen2.5-Math-PRM on MATH steps, Llama Guard per turn on Anthropic red-teaming, the SCM verifier per token on FineHarm), plus an ablation using the generator's own minimum token log-probability (much weaker: ~0.5 power vs >0.9 for the PRM at ε≈0.3). Type 2. A single time-invariant threshold calibrated by conformal risk control or a Hoeffding-Bentkus bound. Metrics: false-alarm rate (share of safe sequences flagged), power, and detection delay defined as "the average fraction of steps the model processes before an alarm is correctly raised". Labels are sequence-level, so delay is relative to sequence length, not to an onset. Competitive with sequential-testing monitors. **[verified, full text]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02510v1)
- **SafeSwitch, Han et al. (arXiv 2502.01042, EMNLP Findings 2025).** Signal: a prober on hidden states that predicts unsafe output before generation, then activates a refusal head. Type 3. Evaluation: about 80% fewer harmful outputs on harmful queries while keeping utility. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01042)
- **Qi et al. 2024, "Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep" (arXiv 2406.05946).** Not a monitor, but shows that alignment mostly shapes the first few output tokens and that harmful continuations follow once those are bypassed, which motivates position-aware monitoring. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05946)
- **Arditi et al. 2024, "Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction" (arXiv 2406.11717).** Not a monitor, but a single residual direction that predicts and controls refusal; often used as a probe direction. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)
- **Bailey et al., "Obfuscated Activations Bypass LLM Latent-Space Defenses" (arXiv 2412.09565, ICLR 2026).** Attacks drive harmfulness-probe recall from 100% to 0% while keeping ~90% jailbreak success; covers SAEs, supervised probes, OOD detectors; finds an "obfuscation tax" on hard tasks. Relevant as the robustness caveat for any activation monitor. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09565)

### Inferences
- The harm-monitoring literature has already moved to streaming evaluation and talks about earliness, but its standard metric is "fraction of the response read before the stop" (SCM, Online Safety Analysis, Schirmer et al.) or "caught within first k sentences" (SentGuard, Qwen3Guard). These metrics reward stopping early in long responses even when harm started early, and they do not separate warnings that come before the onset from detections after it.
- StreamGuard's forecasting framing ("predict, don't react") is the harm-domain counterpart of the paper's Type 3 early-warning monitor and should be cited as such. Its supervision via Monte Carlo rollouts of continuations is close in spirit to the paper's "will this rollout degenerate" labels.
- Qwen3Guard's sentence-level location annotation and the QwenGuardTest response_loc split appear to be the only public harm datasets with annotated onset positions that I found; FineHarm has token labels but they are heuristic (POS-based), so "first harmful token" in FineHarm is approximate.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Constitutional Classifiers full text in this session (fetch was blocked), so the streaming objective details are from memory.
- Definitions of SentGuard's MFDS and SingStreamBench's timeliness metric were not visible in the abstracts.
- I found no harm monitor that reports detection delay in tokens after an annotated first harmful token together with a false-alarm budget on benign prefixes; SingStreamBench may do this, but I could not confirm it.

## Q3. Deception, sandbagging, scheming and high-stakes probes: granularity and metrics

### Takeaway
Deception and high-stakes probes are almost all evaluated as response- or dialogue-level classifiers, using AUROC plus recall at a low false-positive rate set on benign control data (1% FPR on Alpaca-style chat is the common convention). Token-level probe scores are computed but then pooled (mean or max) into one score per response; no work in this group measures when, inside a response, the probe first fires.

### Cited Findings
- **Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025, "Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes" (arXiv 2502.03407, ICML 2025, Apollo Research).** Signal: linear probes on Llama-3.3-70B activations, trained on instruction-contrast pairs. Type 1 (scores pooled over response tokens). Evaluation: response-level AUROC 0.96 to 0.999; with threshold at 1% FPR on unrelated chat data (Alpaca control), catches 95 to 99% of deceptive responses; Roleplaying the weakest (misses ~4% at that threshold). Probe partly fires on "deception-related" text, not only actually deceptive text. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03407); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/goldowsky-dill25a.html); [Apollo blog](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/research/deception-probes)
- **Parrack, Attubato & Heimersheim 2025, "Benchmarking Deception Probes via Black-to-White Performance Boosts" (arXiv 2507.12691).** Signal: the Goldowsky-Dill probes; a white-box monitor sees token-level probe scores, a black-box monitor sees only text. Type 1. Evaluation: the gain of white-box over black-box monitoring across 8 strategies and 5 datasets (insider trading, sandbagging, role-play, backdoored code); boosts are "weak but encouraging"; introduces "deception dilution" (padding with irrelevant truths) as an evasion. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12691)
- **McKenzie et al. 2025, "Detecting High-Stakes Interactions with Activation Probes" (arXiv 2506.10805, NeurIPS 2025).** Signal: probes on Llama-3.3-70B activations trained on ~8,000 synthetic GPT-4o examples. Type 1. Evaluation: dialogue-level AUROC above 0.91 on out-of-distribution real datasets, comparable to prompted or fine-tuned medium-size LLM monitors at about six orders of magnitude less compute; proposes probes as the first stage of a cascade. Authors' blog: at 1% FPR the probe catches ~43% of high-stakes cases vs ~55% for prompted Llama-70B; 32 target-domain labels raise AUROC 0.91 to 0.94. **[verified; blog numbers secondary]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10805); [NeurIPS](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b9501301a7a0f99e57406cc901f4e3ce-Abstract-Conference.html); [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/utcZSRv2JfahD8yfz/detecting-high-stakes-interactions-with-activation-probes)
- **Tillman & Mossing 2025, "Investigating task-specific prompts and sparse autoencoders for activation monitoring" (arXiv 2504.20271, OpenAI).** Signal: raw activation probes, SAE probes, and "prompted probing" (asking the model about the task, then probing). Type 1. Evaluation: classification on moderation, QA (SimpleQA) and sentiment tasks; prompted probing best when inference compute is available, SAE probes better than raw probes when it is not. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20271)
- **MacDiarmid et al. 2024, "Simple probes can catch sleeper agents" (Anthropic).** Signal: linear "defection" probes on residual stream of backdoored models, built from generic contrast pairs. Type 3 in a sense (fires on the prompt before the defecting code is written). Evaluation: AUROC above 99% for detecting when a sleeper agent will defect. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/probes-catch-sleeper-agents)
- **Gupta & Jenner 2025, "RL-Obfuscation" (arXiv 2506.14261).** Shows that RL fine-tuning can teach a model to evade token-level latent monitors while keeping the behaviour; mean/median-pooled probes are evaded more easily than max-pooled or attention probes. Probe robustness caveat. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14261)
- **Bailey et al. (arXiv 2412.09565, ICLR 2026)**, see Q2. Same robustness caveat for harmfulness and other latent monitors. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09565)
- **Kramár et al. 2026 (arXiv 2601.11516)** and **Cunningham et al. 2026 (arXiv 2601.04603)**, see Q2. These are the production "cheap monitor" probe papers from Google DeepMind and Anthropic; both evaluate at exchange/response level with refusal-rate or false-positive constraints. **[verified]** — [arXiv 2601.11516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11516); [arXiv 2601.04603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04603)

### Inferences
- The deception literature's "recall at 1% FPR on a benign control set" is the same idea as the paper's false-alarm budget, which gives a direct precedent for that choice. What these papers do not do is fix the budget per token or per prefix and ask how early in the response the alarm comes.
- Pooling choices (mean vs max over tokens) matter for robustness (RL-Obfuscation) and are a hidden timing decision; a max-pooled response score is equivalent to "did the probe ever fire", which discards when it fired.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published sandbagging-specific probe paper with a clear evaluation protocol beyond the sandbagging dataset in Parrack et al.; UK AISI and Apollo posts on sandbagging probes exist but I did not verify them in this session.
- I did not verify Apollo's or Anthropic's 2025-2026 follow-up deception probe posts beyond the ones above.

## Q4. Probes that predict a property of the future continuation (correctness, reasoning failure, looping) from the current hidden state

### Takeaway
There is a growing set of Type 3 probes that predict future properties from current activations: future tokens (Future Lens), response length and number of steps (Emergent Response Planning), final CoT success (Knowing Before Saying), correctness of upcoming intermediate answers (Reasoning Models Know When They're Right), arithmetic errors (Sun et al.), and loops (Word Salad Chopper, Circular Reasoning). These are evaluated mostly as classification accuracy/F1/AUROC at a fixed position or per chunk, and only the circular-reasoning paper pairs a probe with a change-point detector for real-time monitoring.

### Cited Findings
- **Future Lens, Pal et al. 2023 (arXiv 2311.04897, CoNLL 2023).** Signal: a single hidden state, decoded into tokens several positions ahead. Type 3 (predicts future tokens). Evaluation: top-k accuracy of future-token prediction. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04897)
- **Dong et al. 2025, "Emergent Response Planning in LLMs" (arXiv 2502.06258, ICML 2025 per my knowledge).** Signal: one-hidden-layer MLP probes on prompt representations across eight 7B/8B models. Type 3. Predicts response length, number of reasoning steps, answer confidence and other global attributes; evaluation with correlation coefficients (regression) and F1 (classification); also tracks how predictability evolves during generation. **[verified abstract; venue from memory]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06258)
- **Afzal, Matthes, Chechik & Ziser 2025, "Knowing Before Saying" (arXiv 2505.24362, ACL Findings 2025).** Failure: unsuccessful zero-shot CoT. Signal: hidden-state probe, even before the first output token. Type 3. Evaluation: classification accuracy at several points of the chain; beats a BERT text baseline; later steps do not reliably improve the probe; early-stopping experiments show truncated CoT still beats no CoT. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24362v2); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/new-sigs/2025.findings-acl.662/)
- **Zhang et al. 2025, "Reasoning Models Know When They're Right: Probing Hidden States for Self-Verification" (arXiv 2504.05419).** Signal: two-layer MLP probe on hidden states at intermediate reasoning steps. Type 3 (hidden states encode correctness of future intermediate answers before they are fully formed). Evaluation: verification accuracy and calibration of intermediate answers; as an early-exit rule it saves 24% of inference tokens without loss of accuracy. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05419v1)
- **Sun, Stolfo & Sachan 2025, "Probing for Arithmetic Errors in Language Models" (arXiv 2507.12379, EMNLP 2025).** Signal: probes on hidden states of Gemma-2B-IT. Type 2 (step-level error detection). Evaluation: >90% accuracy predicting correctness; transfers from 3-digit addition to addition-only GSM8K; used for selective re-prompting of faulty steps. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12379v1)
- **Word Salad Chopper, Xie et al. (arXiv 2511.00536, EMNLP 2025).** Failure: useless self-repetition in reasoning models. Signal: hidden states at the double-newline token after each reasoning chunk, single-layer linear classifier. Type 2, chunk-level, on the fly. Intervention: chop the trace and send a regeneration prompt. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00536v1); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1705/)
- **"Circular Reasoning: Understanding Self-Reinforcing Loops in Large Reasoning Models" (arXiv 2601.05693, January 2026; cited by the paper as Duan et al. 2026; author list not checked).** Signal: linear probe on averaged last-layer hidden states per sentence; score rises sharply just before textual repetition starts. Combined with CUSUM change-point detection for real-time monitoring. Argues semantic circularity precedes explicit textual repetition. Type 2/3. **[verified from search snippet only]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05693v1)
- **Zhang, Zhang & Shmatikov 2026, "Speculative Probing: LLM Monitoring at Speculative-Decoding Cost" (arXiv 2608.28099).** Signal: the speculative-decoding module reused as a sequence classifier with a trained soft prompt over the KV cache. Type 1/2. Four classification tasks on Qwen3.5 and MiniCPM; a search snippet showed ~87 to 89% accuracy on a CoT repetition task for Qwen3.5-4B (unverified). **[verified abstract]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28099)
- **ProcessBench, Zheng et al. (arXiv 2412.06559, ACL 2025).** Failure: reasoning errors in math solutions. Signal: PRMs or prompted critics reading the text. Type 2. Task is to output the index of the **earliest erroneous step** (or −1); 3,400 expert-annotated cases; metric is the harmonic mean of accuracy on erroneous and correct solutions. Explicitly scores only the first error because later steps depend on a wrong premise. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559)
- **Lightman et al. 2023, "Let's Verify Step by Step" (arXiv 2305.20050).** Process reward models trained on step labels (PRM800K); step-level supervision and evaluation via best-of-N. Type 2 at step level. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)
- **E-valuator, Sadhuka et al. (arXiv 2512.03109, ICLR/ICML 2026 listings).** Converts any step-level verifier score (LLM judge, PRM) into a sequential test with provable false-alarm control, valid at every step of an agent trajectory, and can end failing trajectories early. Type 2/3 (predicts trajectory failure online). **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03109v1)
- **Baidya et al. 2026, "When Evidence is Sparse: Weakly Supervised Early Failure Alerting in Dialogs and LLM-Agent Trajectories" (arXiv 2606.05414).** Signal: attention-based predictor over turns trained only from trajectory-level labels, plus a stopping policy (α-STOP) for an accuracy vs earliness trade-off. Type 3 at turn level. Reports that failure evidence occupies only 4.7 to 11.3% of turns and first appears after 59 to 83.6% of a trajectory; evaluates Pareto frontiers (hypervolume) of accuracy vs earliness. A search summary defines earliness as trigger turn over total turns and warns that earliness gains can come from lower coverage. **[verified abstract]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05414)
- **Dubey 2026, "Real-Time Detection and Repair of LLM Agent Failures" (arXiv 2608.02464).** Signal: step telemetry only (no activations, no text). One-class echo-state network with CUSUM alarms. Type 2. Reports 0.71 detection at 5% false-alarm budget (AUROC 0.872) and how the advantage grows with post-onset horizon (+0.09 at ≤3 steps, +0.40 at ≥9 steps). **[verified abstract, single-author preprint]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464)
- **DEER, "Dynamic Early Exit in Reasoning Models" (arXiv 2504.15895).** Uses the confidence of trial answers at reasoning transition points to stop reasoning early. Output-based Type 3 for overthinking. **[known, not re-fetched]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15895)

### Inferences
- The "future property" probes give strong support for the paper's premise that the residual stream carries information about a failure before it appears in text (Afzal et al., Zhang et al., Circular Reasoning). But they evaluate at one fixed position or per chunk with accuracy/AUROC, and they do not report a false-alarm-constrained lead time.
- ProcessBench is the clearest precedent for scoring a monitor by whether it finds the first error, which matches the paper's notion of onset. It is a Type 2 text-based task and has no lead-time notion.
- The agent-monitoring line (E-valuator, Baidya et al., Dubey, Schirmer et al.) is where false-alarm control, detection delay and earliness trade-offs are most formally treated. The paper can borrow its vocabulary (power vs false-alarm rate, delay, earliness vs coverage) from there.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the authors of arXiv 2601.05693; the paper cites it as Duan et al. 2026.
- I did not find a published probe that predicts repetitive degeneration (non-reasoning text loops) with an onset-relative lead time other than the circular-reasoning work; this may be a genuine gap the paper fills.

## Q5. Sentence/chunk-level streaming evaluation, and whether related work argues that response-level metrics hide timing

### Takeaway
Yes. Several 2025-2026 works make the timing argument, but each in one domain and with its own metric. Itkin (2026) argues directly that token-level AUC ignores reaction time and evaluates detection delay at a fixed false-alarm rate for hallucination onset; SentGuard, StreamGuard and SingStreamBench argue that response-level guards are too late or that response labels hide when harm appears; Schirmer et al. and E-valuator formalise false-alarm control and delay for streaming monitors. None of them, as far as I found, evaluates an early-warning monitor by lead time before an annotated onset under a per-token false-alarm budget for degeneration.

### Cited Findings
- Itkin: token-level hallucination detectors "are evaluated as classifiers, by AUC over all tokens"; argues for delay at fixed false-alarm rate and shows "low-false-alarm onset detection is hard" (under a third of onsets caught at FAR 0.01). — [arXiv 2606.12476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12476)
- Schirmer et al. define detection delay as "the average fraction of steps the model processes before an alarm is correctly raised", with false-alarm rate as the share of safe sequences flagged; they use sequence-level labels, so there is no onset. — [arXiv 2607.02510](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02510v1)
- SCM measures earliness as fraction of tokens seen before the stop ("over 80% harmful responses are detected within the first 30% of tokens (18% on average)") and does not quantify the gap between first harmful token and stop. — [arXiv 2506.09996](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09996v3)
- SentGuard evaluates at sentence boundaries with Det@k and a streaming false-positive rate, arguing token-level guards are unstable and full-response guards delay intervention. — [arXiv 2606.02041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02041)
- StreamGuard argues streaming moderation is wrongly framed as boundary detection of an already-unsafe prefix and proposes forecasting future harm instead. — [arXiv 2604.03962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03962)
- SingStreamBench tests that guards stay quiet on benign prefixes and fire promptly on emerging unsafe content. — [arXiv 2608.30703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30703)
- Qwen3Guard reports an exact hit rate against sentence-level unsafe-location annotation and "within first 128 tokens" rates for thinking traces. — [arXiv 2510.14276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14276v1)
- Baidya et al. report accuracy vs earliness Pareto frontiers and note (per a search summary) that earliness can improve simply by triggering on fewer positives. — [arXiv 2606.05414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05414)
- E-valuator gives anytime-valid false-alarm control for step-level monitoring of agents. — [arXiv 2512.03109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03109v1)
- Dubey reports detection at a 5% false-alarm budget broken down by post-onset horizon in steps. — [arXiv 2608.02464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464)
- Obeso et al. evaluate entity-level AUC and recall at 10% FPR and do not measure latency. — [arXiv 2509.03531](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03531v1)
- Goldowsky-Dill et al. set thresholds at 1% FPR on benign control chat and report recall, at response level. — [arXiv 2502.03407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03407)
- ProcessBench scores whether a critic finds the earliest erroneous step. — [arXiv 2412.06559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559)

**Synthesis on how the field evaluates monitors.** Probe papers for hallucination, deception and high-stakes behaviour mostly report one score per response (AUROC, accuracy, F1), with recall at a low false-positive rate on a benign control set as the standard way to express a false-alarm budget. When token scores exist, they are pooled into a response score by mean or max, which throws away when the probe fired. Streaming guardrail work for harmful content does evaluate earliness, but almost always as the fraction of the response read before the stop or as "caught within the first k sentences", and labels are often heuristic or response-level, so a stop that comes after the first harmful token can still count as early. Onset-aware evaluation is rare and recent. ProcessBench scores the first erroneous step, Qwen3Guard and StreamGuard use annotated unsafe locations, and Itkin (2026) evaluates detection delay after hallucination onset at a fixed false-alarm rate and states that token-level AUC hides reaction time. The statistically careful treatment of false alarms (risk control, anytime-valid tests, CUSUM) lives mainly in the agent and safety-monitoring papers (Schirmer et al., E-valuator, Dubey, Circular Reasoning). I found no work that combines all three of the paper's ingredients: an annotated onset, a per-token or per-prefix false-alarm budget, and lead time before onset for an early-warning (Type 3) monitor, in the degeneration setting or any other. Itkin (detection after onset) and StreamGuard (forecasting without onset-relative lead time) are the two closest.
