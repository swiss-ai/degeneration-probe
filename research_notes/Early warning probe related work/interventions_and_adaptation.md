# Acting on a monitor's alarm, and adapting the representation a probe reads

Verification legend. "Verified" means the arXiv id or venue was confirmed by a search or fetch in this session (October 2026). "From memory, not re-checked" means the id and venue come from prior knowledge and were not confirmed in this session; check these before citing.

## (A1) Monitor-triggered interventions: which exist, and which measure the cost of false alarms on healthy outputs?

### Takeaway
There is a clear and growing literature where a detector reading the model (activations or text) gates an action during generation: conditional steering, probe-gated safety heads, calibrated steering with abstention, streaming halt classifiers, reset/backtrack tokens, and probe-based early exit from reasoning. Most of these report some proxy for false-alarm cost (harmless-refusal rate, utility benchmarks, production refusal rate, accuracy kept after early exit), but almost none of them targets repetitive degeneration, and none found here acts on a repetition alarm during generation.

### Cited Findings

Detector-gated steering / representation interventions
- Conditional Activation Steering (CAST, Lee, Padhi et al., IBM; ICLR 2025 Spotlight; arXiv 2409.05907, verified). A "condition vector" read from hidden states decides whether to apply a "behavior vector" (refusal), allowing rules like "if input is about hate speech, refuse". It needs no weight optimization, and the paper's figures report that refusals on harmless prompts stay low while harmful prompts are refused. This is the closest template for "monitor fires, then steer", and it does report the false-alarm side (harmless refusals). — [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/e2dd53601de57c773343a7cdf09fae1c-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2409.05907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05907)
- MERA, "To Steer or Not to Steer? Mechanistic Error Reduction with Abstention for Language Models" (Hedström, Amoukou, Bewley, Mishra, Veloso; ICML 2025, PMLR vol. 267, pp. 22924–22945; arXiv 2510.13290, verified). MERA learns the steering direction and calibrates "when, and how much to steer", abstaining when no confident correction is available, and is presented as "safe, effective, non-degrading error correction" that can be layered on top of other steering methods. The ICML lay summary says a lightweight model first estimates the chance of an error and steering happens only when that chance is high. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/hedstrom25a.html); [arXiv 2510.13290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13290); [ICML poster page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/44524)
- A later paper describes MERA's formulation as finding a minimum-norm steering vector so that the predicted probability of an unwanted output stays below a threshold α, applied only to embeddings whose predicted probability exceeds α. In that paper's toxicity experiments MERA beats ITI but a dynamically scaled variant (DSAS) gives better capability retention at equal toxicity reduction. — [Dynamically Scaled Activation Steering, arXiv 2512.03661](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.03661)
- SafeSwitch (Han et al.; Findings of EMNLP 2025; arXiv 2502.01042, verified; first titled "Internal Activation as the Polar Star for Steering Unsafe LLM Behavior"). A probe on internal states detects harmful intent and switches on a "safety head" only when needed; reports about 80% fewer harmful outputs on harmful queries "while maintaining strong utility", tuning under 6% of parameters. — [arXiv 2502.01042](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01042); [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2025.findings-emnlp.366)
- Inference-Time Intervention (ITI, Li et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2306.03341; from memory, not re-checked) shifts activations of probe-selected attention heads along truthful directions at every token, unconditionally. It is the usual unconditional baseline that MERA and DSAS compare against. — [DSAS, arXiv 2512.03661](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.03661) (confirms ITI is used as baseline)
- Other unconditional steering references (from memory, not re-checked): Representation Engineering (Zou et al., arXiv 2310.01405); Contrastive Activation Addition (Rimsky et al., ACL 2024, arXiv 2312.06681); ActAdd (Turner et al., arXiv 2308.10248).
- Steering itself can cause repetition. A 2026 paper notes that additive interventions push activations off the model's native manifold and risk "incoherent or repetitive outputs", and counters this with norm preservation, clamping, and a repetition penalty. — [Bernoulli sparse steering, arXiv 2607.05615](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05615)

Repetition-specific mechanisms that could serve as intervention targets
- "Repetition Neurons: How Do Language Models Produce Repetitions?" (Hiraoka & Inui, NAACL 2025 short; arXiv 2410.13497, verified). Neurons found by comparing activations before and after repetition onset; their activation grows as the repeat continues, read as the model treating repetition as in-context copying. A follow-up (Doan, Hiraoka, Inui, arXiv 2507.07810) finds the effect of these neurons depends on layer depth and compares them with induction heads for reducing repetition. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-short.41/); [arXiv 2507.07810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07810)
- "Understanding the Repeat Curse in Large Language Models from a Feature Perspective" (arXiv 2504.14218, verified as existing; venue not confirmed). Identifies SAE features that drive repetition and reports RepeatScore, entropy and perplexity when these features are deactivated (Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.1-8B, GPT-2). Whether deactivation is applied conditionally on a detector was not confirmed. — [arXiv 2504.14218](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14218)
- "Interpreting the Repeated Token Phenomenon in Large Language Models" (Yona, Shumailov, Hayes, Barbero, Gandelsman; ICML 2025; arXiv 2503.08908, verified). Links divergence on repeated-word prompts to attention sinks (a first-layer head marks the first token; repeated tokens get marked too) and proposes a targeted patch that fixes it "without hurting overall model performance". — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/yona25a.html); [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/45013)

Stopping or truncating when a monitor fires (streaming guardrails)
- "From Judgment to Interference: Early Stopping LLM Harmful Outputs via Streaming Content Monitoring" (Li et al., NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2506.09996, verified). Argues moderators trained on full responses degrade on partial text; trains a Streaming Content Monitor with both response-level and token-level labels (FineHarm, 29K pairs) and reports macro F1 above 0.95 after reading about 18% of tokens on average. — [arXiv 2506.09996](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09996); [NeurIPS 2025 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116186)
- Qwen3Guard Technical Report (arXiv 2510.14276, verified). "Stream Qwen3Guard" scores every generated token for safety in real time (0.6B/4B/8B). The paper lists reduced generalization of the stream variant in ambiguous contexts. A third-party summary reports catching unsafe content within the first 128 tokens in about 66.8% of thinking-trace cases; not checked against the paper. — [arXiv 2510.14276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14276)
- Constitutional Classifiers (Anthropic; arXiv 2501.18837, verified). Reports an absolute 0.38% increase in production-traffic refusals and 23.7% inference overhead; benchmark FPRs of 1.50% on MMLU-Chemistry but 26.05% on GPQA-Chemistry. The streaming output classifier that halts generation is part of the system, but its halting mechanics were not read here. — [arXiv 2501.18837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18837)
- Constitutional Classifiers++ (Anthropic; arXiv 2601.04603, verified). A cheap linear probe on Claude's activations screens all traffic and escalates flagged exchanges to a probe-classifier ensemble; 40x lower compute than the baseline exchange classifier at a 0.05% refusal rate on production traffic, about 1% compute overhead. — [arXiv 2601.04603](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04603); [Anthropic blog](https://www.anthropic.com/research/next-generation-constitutional-classifiers?cam=claude)
- Real-time hallucinated-entity probes (Obeso, Arditi, Ferrando, Freeman, Holmes, Nanda; arXiv 2509.03531, verified; venue not confirmed). Their intervention is selective answering on SimpleQA: generation halts when any token's score passes threshold t and the model outputs "I don't know". Lowering t raises conditional accuracy and lowers the attempt rate. The limitations section states that meaningfully reducing hallucinations through hallucination-aware sampling "requires sacrificing ∼50% of correct answers". This is a rare explicit statement of the false-alarm cost on healthy outputs. — [arXiv 2509.03531 (HTML v1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03531v1)

Resampling / backtracking
- "Backtracking Improves Generation Safety" (Zhang et al., Meta/CMU; ICLR 2025 oral; arXiv 2409.14586, verified). The model emits a [RESET] token after starting an unsafe response, discards everything up to it, and regenerates; at most one backtrack per generation. Llama-3-8B unsafe rate drops from 6.1% to 1.5% "without regression in helpfulness". The detector here is the model itself, trained to emit [RESET]. — [arXiv 2409.14586](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.14586); [ICLR 2025 oral](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/oral/31902)
- A follow-up (arXiv 2503.08919, verified as existing; title not captured) reverts to a safer intermediate state instead of a full reset, to correct problematic segments in long outputs without discarding everything. — [arXiv 2503.08919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08919)

Adaptive decoding / temperature
- EDT, Entropy-based Dynamic Temperature sampling (Zhang, Bao, Huang; arXiv 2403.14541, verified; venue not confirmed). Sets temperature per step from the entropy of the next-token distribution. Reports gains on four benchmarks. The trigger is output entropy, not an internal probe. — [arXiv 2403.14541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14541)
- Adaptive temperature for code (Zhu et al., "Hot or Cold?", AAAI 2024, arXiv 2309.02772; from memory, not re-checked).
- Learning to break the loop (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2206.02369; from memory, not re-checked). Training-time fix for self-reinforcing repetition, not a monitor-triggered action, but the standard repetition-degeneration reference.

Early exit from reasoning
- "Reasoning Models Know When They're Right: Probing Hidden States for Self-Verification" (Zhang et al.; arXiv 2504.05419, verified; venue not confirmed). A hidden-state probe verifies intermediate answers with calibrated scores; thresholding it cuts inference tokens by 24% without loss of accuracy. — [arXiv 2504.05419](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05419v1)
- LYNX (arXiv 2512.05325, verified as existing). Probe on hidden states at cue tokens ("wait", "hmm") decides exit, with split conformal prediction controlling premature exits; probe calibrated once on a math corpus and reused across benchmarks. — [arXiv 2512.05325](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.05325)
- Related early-exit methods found (existence verified, details from abstracts only): EAT, entropy after </think> (arXiv 2509.26522); NEAT, neuron-based early exit (arXiv 2602.02010); SpecExit (ICML 2026); BLADE (arXiv 2607.28966). — [EAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26522v1); [NEAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02010v1); [SpecExit](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/66249); [BLADE](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.28966)
- DEER, Dynamic Early Exit in Reasoning Models (Yang et al., arXiv 2504.15895; from memory, not re-checked).

### Inferences
- MERA is a steering-with-abstention method, i.e. a topic (A) work. It does not adapt the representation the probe reads. If the paper currently cites Hedström et al. 2025 to support its LoRA adaptation, that citation belongs in the intervention paragraph instead, and the adaptation claim needs a different anchor (see B1, Obeso et al. 2025 is the closest match).
- The intervention literature supports the paper's framing that an early monitor is useful because it enables action. CAST, SafeSwitch and MERA all gate an internal intervention on an internal detector; SCM, Qwen3Guard and Constitutional Classifiers halt mid-stream; backtracking and LYNX resample or exit. The paper can position the degeneration probe as the trigger in exactly this pattern.
- False-alarm cost is reported in most of these works, but in task-specific currencies: harmless-refusal rate (CAST, Constitutional Classifiers), utility or helpfulness benchmarks (SafeSwitch, backtracking), retained accuracy (early exit), and fraction of correct answers lost (Obeso et al., about 50%). The paper's argument that a degeneration false alarm only costs stopping or steering a healthy response is consistent with this, and could be made concrete by reporting what a false alarm does to a healthy response under each action (truncation length lost, quality after steering).
- Steering can itself induce repetition (arXiv 2607.05615). This is a reason to prefer conditional (alarm-gated) steering for degeneration, since unconditional steering may cause the failure it is meant to prevent.
- The repetition-mechanism papers (repetition neurons, Repeat Curse SAE features, attention-sink patch) supply candidate intervention targets that an alarm could switch on, but none of them is used conditionally on an early-warning signal in the sources read.

### Gaps
- No paper found that triggers an intervention (steering, temperature change, resample) from a repetition or degeneration alarm during generation. This looks like an open gap the paper can claim, but the search was not exhaustive.
- Whether MERA's error estimator is a linear probe on activations, and whether MERA reports the effect of steering on inputs that were already correct, could not be confirmed from the abstract page; the full paper was not read.
- Halting mechanics and streaming false-positive rates for Constitutional Classifiers' output classifier were not found in a primary source.
- Detailed table for Obeso et al.'s selective-answering experiment (Appendix H) was not read.

## (A2) How do monitors choose operating points? Is there work on choosing the false-alarm budget for streaming LLM monitors?

### Takeaway
The dominant convention in activation-probe monitoring is a fixed low false-positive rate (usually 1%) calibrated on benign "control" traffic, and production systems report refusal rate on real traffic as the operating metric. Conformal calibration appears for early-exit decisions. No source found argues for a specific budget for streaming monitors from first principles, so a 5% response-level budget justified by cost asymmetry is a defensible but self-argued choice.

### Cited Findings
- Apollo Research, "Detecting Strategic Deception with Linear Probes" (Goldowsky-Dill et al., ICML 2025; arXiv 2502.03407, verified). Threshold calibrated to 1% FPR on unrelated chat data (control); at that point the probe flags about 95–99% of deceptive responses, AUROC 0.96–0.999. The authors still judge it insufficient as a robust defense. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/goldowsky-dill25a.html); [arXiv 2502.03407](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2502.03407)
- "Detecting high-stakes interactions with activation probes" (McKenzie et al.; the source read is a LessWrong post; arXiv id believed to be 2506.10805, not re-checked). At 1% FPR probes catch about 43% of high-stakes cases vs about 55% for a prompted Llama-70B; the authors suggest probes as cheap first-stage filters in a cascade. — [LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/utcZSRv2JfahD8yfz/detecting-high-stakes-interactions-with-activation-probes)
- Constitutional Classifiers report the operating point as the increase in production refusals (0.38% absolute), and Constitutional Classifiers++ as a 0.05% refusal rate on production traffic with a probe-first cascade. — [arXiv 2501.18837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18837); [arXiv 2601.04603](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04603)
- Constitutional Classifiers also show that FPR depends strongly on the benign distribution (1.50% on MMLU-Chemistry vs 26.05% on GPQA-Chemistry), so an FPR budget is only meaningful relative to a stated healthy distribution. — [arXiv 2501.18837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18837)
- LYNX uses split conformal prediction to control the rate of premature exits, i.e. a statistically calibrated false-alarm guarantee for an in-generation stopping rule. — [LYNX, arXiv 2512.05325](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05325v1)
- Obeso et al. expose the operating point as a threshold sweep (attempt rate vs conditional accuracy) instead of fixing one. — [arXiv 2509.03531](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03531v1)
- Formal background (from memory, not re-checked): Neyman-Pearson classification fixes a type-I error bound and maximizes power (Tong, Feng, Li, Science Advances 2018); Conformal Language Modeling calibrates a sampling and stopping rule with coverage guarantees (Quach et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2306.10193); cost-sensitive thresholding follows Elkan 2001 (IJCAI).

### Inferences
- The paper's 5% response-level budget is looser than the 1% convention in deception and high-stakes probing. The cost asymmetry argument (a false alarm on degeneration only stops or steers a healthy response, while in deception or misuse a miss is the expensive error) is the natural justification, and it inverts the usual safety framing in a way worth stating explicitly. Reporting results at 1% as well would let readers compare with Apollo and McKenzie et al.
- Calibrating at the response level is the right unit for a streaming monitor. A per-token FPR compounds over long responses because the alarm is a maximum over many tokens; SCM and Qwen3Guard face the same issue, and Obeso et al. halt on "any token" above threshold. The sources do not discuss this compounding explicitly, so the paper can make the point itself.
- The FPR-depends-on-distribution result from Constitutional Classifiers supports reporting the false-alarm budget per dataset or domain, especially for held-out domains.
- Probe-first cascades (Constitutional Classifiers++, McKenzie et al.) offer a second framing for the operating point. The probe can run at a loose budget if a more expensive check (or a cheap reversible action) follows the alarm.

### Gaps
- No work found that derives or empirically studies the choice of false-alarm budget for streaming LLM monitors specifically.
- The McKenzie et al. arXiv id and venue were not confirmed in this session.

## (B1) Probes trained jointly with LoRA / ReFT / fine-tuning, and how fine-tuning changes linear readability

### Takeaway
The closest prior work to the paper's setup is Obeso et al. 2025, which trains LoRA adapters in all layers below a linear head jointly with that head and finds a consistent AUC gain over plain linear probes, with a regularizer to limit behavior drift. Mechanistic work (Prakash et al.) supports the reading that fine-tuning strengthens existing mechanisms, while obfuscation work (Bailey et al.) shows the same tool can push readability the other way. No source found reports the paper's specific finding that adaptation raises coverage without moving the first alarm earlier.

### Cited Findings
- Obeso et al., "Real-Time Detection of Hallucinated Entities in Long-Form Generation" (arXiv 2509.03531, verified; first posted Aug 2025, revised Feb 2026). LoRA adapters are inserted into all layers before a linear value head at layer ⌊0.95 × L⌋ and trained jointly with the head. Loss is (1 − λ)·probe loss + λ·regularizer, with LM loss (λ = 0.01, default) or KL to the frozen model. LongFact AUC rises from 0.8535 to 0.8938 (Llama-3.1-8B) and 0.8667 to 0.9048 (Llama-3.3-70B). KL regularization at λ_KL = 0.5 keeps AUC at 0.8898 while cutting KL drift from 0.1048 to 0.0046 and restoring win rate from 35.9% to 52.8%. LoRA rank was not visible in the part read. No analysis of detection timing with vs without LoRA was found in the first 100k characters. — [arXiv 2509.03531 (HTML v1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03531v1); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03531)
- "Fine-Tuning Enhances Existing Mechanisms: A Case Study on Entity Tracking" (Prakash, Rott Shaham, Haklay, Belinkov, Bau; ICLR 2024; arXiv 2402.14811, verified). Base and math-fine-tuned Llama-7B variants use the same entity-tracking circuit; the gain comes mostly from better handling of positional information, i.e. fine-tuning strengthens an existing mechanism instead of building a new one. — [arXiv 2402.14811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14811); [project page](https://finetuning.baulab.info/)
- "Obfuscated Activations Bypass LLM Latent-Space Defenses" (Bailey et al., ICLR 2026; arXiv 2412.09565, verified). Adversarial suffixes, data poisoning and model fine-tuning can produce activations that evade probes, SAEs and OOD detectors, reducing harmfulness-probe recall from 100% to 0% at 90% jailbreak success; evasion has an "obfuscation tax" on hard tasks (SQL). Whether LoRA specifically was used was not confirmed. — [arXiv 2412.09565](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.09565); [ICLR 2026 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/ed687a5f52b651b19e7c18f702907b8b-Abstract-Conference.html)
- "Trojan-Speak: Bypassing Constitutional Classifiers with No Jailbreak Tax via Adversarial Finetuning" (arXiv 2603.29038, verified as existing; details not read) is a further example of fine-tuning used to defeat a classifier-based monitor. — [arXiv 2603.29038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29038)
- SafeSwitch fine-tunes a small set of parameters (under 6%) for a safety head that is activated by a probe; adaptation and probing are combined, but the adapter serves the response, not the probe. — [arXiv 2502.01042](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01042)
- From memory, not re-checked: ReFT, representation fine-tuning (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2404.03592); LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv 2106.09685); LoFiT, localized fine-tuning of representations (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2406.01563); "Fine-Tuning can Distort Pretrained Features and Underperform Out-of-Distribution" (Kumar et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv 2202.10054), which motivates linear-probe-then-fine-tune; "Mechanistically analyzing the effects of fine-tuning on procedurally defined tasks" (Jain et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2311.12786), which argues fine-tuning learns a thin "wrapper" over existing capabilities; RL-Obfuscation (Gupta & Jenner, arXiv 2506.14261), RL fine-tuning that evades latent monitors.

### Inferences
- Obeso et al. should be the primary citation for "train LoRA jointly with a linear probe, keep the head linear". Their architecture (LoRA in every block below the head, linear head) matches the paper's design, and their gain (about +0.04 AUC) is similar in kind to the paper's coverage gain. The paper differs in restricting LoRA to q/v at rank 16 and in measuring warning coverage and first-alarm timing, which Obeso et al. do not report as far as read.
- Prakash et al. and Jain et al. support the paper's interpretation that LoRA makes an existing signal easier to read linearly. The paper's own evidence (no earlier first alarm) fits this view, because if LoRA created new predictive information one would expect some earlier detections.
- Bailey et al. cut both ways. They show that fine-tuning can move probe readability a lot, which supports the plausibility of LoRA raising readability. But they also show that a jointly trained adapter could in principle shape the representation toward the label in ways that do not reflect the base model's state. Two checks would address this: (1) state whether the LoRA is applied only on the monitor's forward pass or also changes the generated tokens, and (2) report behavior drift (KL or output change), as Obeso et al. do with their KL regularizer.
- The "coverage up, timing unchanged" result appears to be new. It is worth stating as a finding, together with the interpretation that adaptation helps the probe read a signal that was already present at the same position.

### Gaps
- No source found that reports adaptation improving detection without changing detection time; this could not be confirmed or ruled out beyond the works read.
- LoRA rank and target modules in Obeso et al. were not seen in the part of the paper read.
- No work found that interprets what a probe-coupled LoRA changes in the representation (e.g. comparing directions, subspace overlap, or whether the base-model probe direction is preserved).

## (B2) Where in depth are linear signals about future outcomes most readable?

### Takeaway
General representation-quality and probing work finds intermediate layers usually give the most linearly useful features, and several future-oriented probing works read response-level properties from prompt or early-generation activations. Practical streaming probes, however, sometimes sit near the top (Obeso et al. use about 95% depth), so the best layer depends on task and on whether an adapter is used.

### Cited Findings
- "Layer by Layer: Uncovering Hidden Representations in Language Models" (Skean et al., ICML 2025; arXiv 2502.02013, verified). Across 32 MTEB tasks and several architectures (transformers, state-space models), intermediate layers consistently give stronger features than the final layer, explained by a trade-off between compression and signal preservation. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/skean25a.html); [arXiv 2502.02013](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02013v2)
- "Emergent Response Planning in LLMs" (Dong et al.; arXiv 2502.06258, verified; venue believed ICML 2025, not re-checked). Probes on prompt representations predict global properties of the response before it is generated: structure (length, number of reasoning steps), content (multiple-choice answer at the end), and behavior (answer confidence, factual consistency). They also track how this evolves with model size and during generation. — [arXiv 2502.06258](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06258v3)
- Obeso et al. place their head at ⌊0.95 × L⌋ with LoRA below it; no layer sweep was seen in the portion read. — [arXiv 2509.03531](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03531v1)
- Repetition neurons have depth-dependent effects on in-context learning (Doan, Hiraoka, Inui). — [arXiv 2507.07810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07810)
- The repeated-token failure is traced to a first-layer attention head plus a later neuron that builds the attention sink, so part of the repetition mechanism lives very early in depth. — [ICML 2025 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/45013)
- From memory, not re-checked: "The Geometry of Truth" (Marks & Tegmark, COLM 2024, arXiv 2310.06824) finds truth directions clearest in middle layers; ITI (arXiv 2306.03341) selects heads mostly in middle layers; "Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction" (Arditi et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2406.11717) extracts the direction from middle layers; "LLMs Know More Than They Show" (Orgad et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.02707) finds truthfulness signals concentrated at exact-answer tokens and can predict error types; "Future Lens" (Pal et al., CoNLL 2023, arXiv 2311.04897) and "Do Language Models Plan Ahead for Future Tokens?" (Wu et al., COLM 2024, arXiv 2404.00859) read later tokens from a single hidden state, best from middle layers.

### Inferences
- A mid-depth peak for the degeneration probe would match the general pattern (Skean et al., truthfulness and refusal probing). If the paper finds the best layer is high, Obeso et al. give a precedent from streaming hallucination detection.
- "Emergent response planning" gives a direct precedent for the claim that response-level outcomes are linearly readable before they surface, which supports an early-warning framing for degeneration.
- Because the repetition mechanism involves both very early components (attention-sink head) and neurons whose effect depends on depth, it is plausible that a degeneration signal is spread across depth, which would also explain why LoRA on blocks below the probe layer helps gather it.

### Gaps
- No probing study found that targets future repetitive degeneration specifically and reports a layer sweep.
- Layer-specific results for Marks & Tegmark, Arditi et al., Orgad et al. and Future Lens are stated from memory and were not re-checked in this session.
